# Derived Information Governance — Cross-Domain Failure Boundaries and Rationale

**Purpose:** Retain the substantive architectural distinctions from the original derived-information adversarial integration work, without its test-verdict narrative. The complete source remains in Concord-1.3.

## 1. Shared-subject genomic inference

A participant's legitimate consent to Health use of their genomic data does **not** constitute a relative's consent to participant-specific inference or disclosure about that relative. Relational Information Rights and Shared-Subject Claims address the independently affected person. A legitimate population finding need not authorise person-specific operationalisation; Health notification to a relative requires its own competence, validation and authority.

## 2. Withdrawal after irreversible learning

Withdrawal can end **future-use authority** without making a model, published aggregate or human memory forget information already learned. Source deletion is not proof of model unlearning. Subsequent participant-specific use, re-querying, disclosure or exploitation may be restricted even when exact removal of diffuse learned contribution is infeasible. Retraining/unlearning decisions must account for feasibility and consequence, not assume automatic reversal. Preserve withdrawal state and provenance.

## 3. Anonymous group harm and consequential classification

**Anonymous information can cause consequential harm without becoming personal information.** A genuinely non-identifying group statistic can still be misused to disadvantage group members. The problem may lie in fairness, rights, discrimination, context and **authority to use the statistic for a consequential decision**, rather than in individual re-identification. Group correlation is not individual fact, and prediction does not grant decision authority.

## 4. Ephemeral inference

**No stored profile ≠ no derived-information event.** A system that infers vulnerability transiently and manipulates a participant cannot evade protection merely by not persisting a profile. Govern the inference/use operation, its purpose and effects; a derived-information object may be transient.

## 5. Human memory after withdrawal

A researcher who legitimately learned a sensitive fact cannot necessarily erase memory. Withdrawal can still end future permission to disclose, formally reuse, re-query or exploit the fact. Information governance cannot depend solely on technical deletion.

## 6. Cross-civilisational protection mismatch

A transfer from civilisation A (protected information) to B (locally public information) must not silently downgrade protection to B's standard. Nor does A automatically acquire sovereignty over B's entire information regime. **Cross-boundary transfer requires a legitimate bounded interface state; it does not automatically select either side's entire protection regime.**

The interface may define purpose, minimum handling conditions, onward disclosure, retention, dispute route, refusal/alternative and unresolved classification differences. Procedural interoperability does not mean identical substantive law; authority for actual transfer and downstream use must remain grounded.

## 7. Public-source reconstruction

Public availability of individual source facts does not automatically authorise combining them into an intimate protected cognitive/health inference. New protection can arise through correlation/recombination and derivation without giving the participant ownership over the original public facts.

## 8. Multi-hop protection laundering

Protected source S may become bounded derivative D1, abstract D2, and later recombine with X to reconstruct S-like protected meaning. Each material transformation requires assessment of its current protection and authority state. **Onward legitimacy is not automatically transitive.** Protection may re-emerge; permanent universal taint is not required.

## 9. Derived evidence in adjudication

Evidence derived from a protected source may be relevant to a judicial case while also implicating privacy. Privacy is not a universal evidence veto, and evidential relevance does not itself grant access, use or disclosure authority. Use minimum-necessary projection where legitimate; substantive evidentiary decisions and due process belong to Judiciary and Law.

## 10. Group model to individual decision

A valid population association does not establish individual diagnosis or automatically authorise an adverse participant-specific decision. The move from group evidence to individual classification is a **new consequential decision**, requiring appropriate context, domain-specific evidence thresholds and legitimate authority.

## Architecture decomposition and domain interfaces

Retain distinct owners for **source state → operation → derivative → claims → protection → authority → use → transition → exposure → remedy**. These are interacting responsibilities, not one central information-governance controller.

Relevant domain interfaces include familial Health/Law notification; Research withdrawal and unlearning; Judiciary's use of protected derivatives; intercivilisational information-transfer protocols; AI ephemeral-inference controls; and discrimination protections for anonymous group statistics. These interfaces may require domain-specific design, but their existence does not justify silently inventing a new general authority layer.
