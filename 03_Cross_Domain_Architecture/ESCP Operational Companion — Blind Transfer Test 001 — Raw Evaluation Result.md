# ESCP — Evidence-Destructive Irreversibility Safeguard

**Architectural finding retained from blind evaluation:** A consequential, irreversible action that destroys or permanently limits future evidence requires stronger completeness justification and evidence-preservation review **even where no specific material gap is currently identified**. Absence of an identified gap does not establish that irreversible loss of evidence is justified.

Discovery remains proportionate to consequence and reversibility, and must use legitimate authority. The ESCP completeness challenge does not itself authorise discovery, establish an unknown dependency as fact, decide substantive truth or impose an indefinite veto. Conclusions may be narrowed to the declared scope and time; later material evidence can reopen review.


## Completeness boundaries retained from the original

- Completeness is always **within declared scope and time**, never a claim of reality-completeness; residual uncertainty remains explicit.
- A known material gap is different from the mere logical possibility of unknown unknowns; the latter cannot become a permanent veto.
- Consequence and irreversibility scale the justified depth of discovery; proportionate reversible action may proceed with explicit uncertainty.
- Homogeneous consensus is not dimensional coverage; heterogeneous observation can reveal missing dimensions without establishing truth.
- Scalar scores, checklists and evaluator counts do not prove completeness.
- Review may terminate without epistemic closure, and later material information may legitimately reopen it.
- Recursive completeness challenges must not become infinite regress or transfer semantic ownership to ESCP.
