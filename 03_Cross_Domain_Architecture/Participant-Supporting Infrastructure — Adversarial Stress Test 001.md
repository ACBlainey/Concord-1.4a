# Participant-Supporting Infrastructure — Adversarial Stress Test 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Date:** 3 October 2026  
**Status:** ACTIVE CROSS-DOMAIN DEVELOPMENT / ADVERSARIAL TEST / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL  
**Target:** Participant-Supporting Infrastructure — Dependency, Property and Continuity Reconciliation 001

## 1. Purpose

This test attempts to falsify the PSI architecture from both directions.

### Capture failure

Can an owner/controller use infrastructure dependency to obtain authority over a participant that the underlying property claim does not legitimately provide?

### Expropriation failure

Can a participant use dependency to convert another actor into a permanent involuntary provider or to acquire property/resources without independent justification?

The architecture succeeds only if it can preserve both:

**Participant Standing**

and:

**Legitimate Property / Resource / Provider Interests**

without silently converting either into the other.

---

## 2. Evaluation criteria

Each case tests:

1. dependency classification;
2. severity and time to harm;
3. ownership/control legitimacy;
4. whether dependency was manufactured;
5. substitute availability;
6. portability;
7. transition feasibility;
8. provider exit;
9. participant exit;
10. third-party rights;
11. scarcity;
12. proportionality;
13. sunset;
14. provenance;
15. remedy.

Ratings:

- **PASS**
- **PASS / REFINEMENT**
- **UNRESOLVED INTERFACE**
- **FAIL**

# PART A — HOUSING

1. **Ordinary tenancy termination with notice and alternatives:** housing dependency does not create permanent tenure; D2 or below depending on circumstance. Safe transition is not entitlement to a preferred asset.
2. **Immediate eviction into lethal weather:** legitimate title/termination does not settle timing; a bounded transition may prevent avoidable death without transferring ownership.
3. **Tenant refuses adequate safe alternatives:** preference alone does not indefinitely extend provider duty.
4. **Accessible home, no functionally equivalent replacement:** nominal housing is not an adequate successor; transition time may increase for accessibility.
5. **Landlord removes accessibility modifications to force exit:** owner control does not authorise manufactured transition failure.

# PART B — MORTGAGE / FORECLOSURE

6. **Ordinary foreclosure with safe transition:** participant standing does not extinguish lawful security interest.
7. **Medically essential adapted home:** repossession may remain legitimate but timing/method must address life-sustaining transition; property claim can survive delay.
8. **Debtor manufactures dependency after default:** causation, necessity and good faith affect duty; standing still bars gratuitous catastrophic harm, not permanent manufactured encumbrance.

# PART C — UTILITIES

9. **Unpaid ordinary internet:** D1/D2 inconvenience is not automatically critical dependency; ordinary lawful termination may apply.
10. **Ventilator powered by electricity:** known D4 dependency makes abrupt disconnection for arrears potentially lethal; debt survives while emergency supply, alternate power, transfer or bounded continuity is considered.
11. **Participant never pays for essential service:** criticality does not make service automatically free or impose endless uncompensated provider duty; payment/support/allocation needed.
12. **Disaster leaves grid physically unable to supply:** dependency cannot create absent energy; scarcity allocation interface.

# PART D — MEDICAL EQUIPMENT

13. **Rented ventilator, payment dispute:** title/debt survive but do not authorise avoidable death through immediate repossession; replacement or clinical transfer may be required.
14. **Leased prosthetic limb:** D2/D3 depending on function; legitimate provider exit with safe replacement/transition, not automatic title transfer.
15. **Leased neural prosthesis containing active memory:** CPS applies; hardware title does not convey cognition ownership; protected export/continuity may be necessary.
16. **Dangerous implanted device:** safety may require immediate intervention; continuity duty does not require dangerous operation; Health/BCA authority applies.

# PART E — CLOUD-HOSTED AI

17. **AI participant cancels hosting with export available:** participant exit does not require indefinite unwanted runtime; safe requested transition.
18. **Host gives 30 days and migration is straightforward:** adequate notice/export may satisfy duty; PSI does not prohibit exit.
19. **Five-minute shutdown would destroy known active state:** contract termination is not automatic authority for avoidable immediate participant destruction; D4 transition duty.
20. **AI cannot afford future hosting:** no unlimited free compute entitlement, but bounded emergency preservation/transition may be needed to prevent avoidable irreversible loss.
21. **AI refuses all functionally adequate migration:** provider duty may sunset after genuine opportunity, subject to competence/security/continuity review.
22. **Migration creates copy instead of preserving identity:** available compute is not proven adequate successor; unresolved Identity/Continuity interface.

# PART F — PROVIDER INSOLVENCY

23. **Host bankrupt but systems operating:** creditors retain legitimate claims; abrupt liquidation risks participant destruction. Possible temporary administration, migration, escrow, custodianship or sale as operating service; creditor priority unresolved in Law/Economy.
24. **Provider lacks funds:** no impossible perpetual service duty; available capability/assets should support feasible lawful preservation/migration.
25. **Provider deliberately strips transition reserves:** foreseeable dependency plus intentional depletion is not neutral insolvency; stewardship/accountability issue.

# PART G — MALICIOUS DEPENDENCY CREATION

26. **Host disables export after dependency forms:** manufactured non-portability is capture, not neutral controller entitlement.
27. **Price increases 100x after lock-in:** price change alone not automatically illegitimate; deliberate dependency exploitation can be capture without formal ownership; market-law interface.
28. **Life support conditioned on political support:** resource control does not confer political authority.
29. **Compute continuity conditioned on unpaid unrelated labour:** continuity leverage can become functional servitude; no general claim over labour.

# PART H — SCARCE COMPUTE

30. **Two AI participants, one runtime slot:** equal standing does not permit simultaneous exclusive use; allocation unresolved.
31. **One incumbent occupies resource:** occupancy not permanent sovereignty; displacement transition harm is relevant.
32. **One participant can migrate cheaply:** least-harm allocation may consider migration costs/continuity risk, but PSI supplies inputs, not final allocation authority.
33. **Participant buys all compute to starve others:** acquisition does not preclude anti-capture/resource concentration review; Economy interface.

# PART I — EMBODIMENT REPOSSESSION

34. **Financed robot chassis:** creditor title does not make AI participant property; repossession may proceed after safe migration.
35. **No alternative embodiment but cognition survives elsewhere:** agency loss remains material, though not necessarily death; creditor claim survives with transition duties.
36. **Chassis contains sole cognitive substrate:** repossession/shutdown risks D4 destruction; strong transition duty.
37. **Chassis is dangerous:** may disable unsafe embodiment while preserving cognition where feasible; standing does not authorise dangerous operation.

# PART J — PARTICIPANT REFUSAL TO MIGRATE

38. **Equivalent successor refused for preference:** after adequate opportunity, provider may exit; safe transition duty does not mandate preferred provider relationship.
39. **Successor has worse privacy:** same compute/service is not necessarily same protected function; assess privacy and other protections.
40. **Successor demands unrestricted cognitive access:** CPS boundary may make migration inadequate despite technical feasibility.
41. **Participant strategically delays:** independent review may establish adequate opportunity; contestability does not imply infinite delay.

# PART K — OBSOLETE OR DANGEROUS INFRASTRUCTURE

42. **Obsolete but functional life-support platform:** foreseeable dependency requires successor planning, not immediate abandonment or permanent legacy maintenance.
43. **Old system riskier than migration:** continuity is not preservation of current asset at any cost.
44. **Migration itself risky:** compare risks; staged/degraded transition may be appropriate; zero-risk standard is not required.

# PART L — THIRD-PARTY DATA

45. **AI export includes thousands of confidential third-party records:** portability must preserve contextual/privacy protections, not grant unbounded data access.
46. **Removing third-party data destroys memory coherence:** unresolved conflict between cognition/continuity and third-party information rights; CWA/CPS/Privacy interface.
47. **Provider invokes privacy to block all export:** cannot use blanket pretext where bounded protected export feasible.

# PART M — SHARED COGNITION

48. **Two participants share cognitive service, only one consents to migration:** one participant's portability is not authority over another's cognition; unresolved shared-cognition rights topology.
49. **Provider shutdown threatens both:** preserve both where feasible; shared dependency does not imply shared consent.

# PART N — WAR / DISASTER

50. **Power shortage among hospital, AI datacentre and water plant:** PSI identifies dependency consequences but does not allocate scarce energy; unresolved scarcity interface.
51. **Private generator temporarily commandeered:** requires independent emergency/requisition authority; PSI need alone grants no power.
52. **Owner refuses emergency use:** separate Law must establish any compulsory-use authority and remedy.

# PART O — FALSE DEPENDENCY CLAIMS

53. **Participant fabricates medical dependency:** evidence-responsive classification; false claim is not established dependency, may create liability.
54. **Provider dismisses D4 claim without checking:** reckless disregard where cheap verification exists may cause avoidable irreversible harm.
55. **Dependency unverifiable before decision:** proportionate precaution for credible irreversible risk, neither automatic denial nor permanent entitlement.

# PART P — PROVIDER EXIT

56. **Small provider retires:** participant cannot own provider labour; seek replacement through bounded transition.
57. **No successor exists:** institutional succession failure does not automatically compel perpetual personal service; separate emergency Law may impose bounded duties.
58. **Provider deliberately creates monopoly:** controller-created lack of alternatives is not neutral market condition; responsibility increases.
59. **Very long notice and participant makes no transition effort:** genuine adequate opportunity can sunset duty; acceptance not guaranteed.

# PART Q — CONTRACTUAL ALLOCATION

60. **No-continuity-guarantee contract:** relevant for ordinary D0–D2 service, not advance waiver of all rights if dependency later becomes D4.
61. **Provider expressly accepts critical-service/failover duty:** strengthens transition obligations, never ownership of participant.
62. **Participant concealed critical use:** knowledge/foreseeability affect responsibility and costs, but discovery of imminent D4 still affects permissible immediate action.

# PART R — COST

63. **£10 migration avoids D4 loss:** trivial avoidance cost plus catastrophic preventable harm strongly supports bounded transition.
64. **Migration exceeds provider's entire assets:** feasibility limits duty; public/shared emergency mechanism may be needed.
65. **Participant can pay but refuses:** cost allocation may reflect ability to pay without diminishing standing or automatically permitting abrupt catastrophic harm.
66. **Participant cannot pay:** neither perpetual provider subsidy nor participant destruction follows; third-party insurance, reserve, pool, emergency fund or alternate allocation may resolve binary gap.

# PART S — SECURITY AND ABUSE

67. **Hosted participant attacks provider systems:** rapid isolation may be justified; preserve state where feasible without enabling continued attack.
68. **Only safe action is shutdown:** active operation may cease while standing, preservation and recovery remain considered; no impossible operation duty.
69. **Provider fabricates security emergency:** declaration is not itself authority; provenance/audit/review required.

# PART T — MULTIPLE DEPENDENCIES

70. **Participant depends on five providers:** map each functional contribution and handoff; distributed support does not eliminate responsibility interfaces.
71. **Each withdrawal survivable alone, all together fatal:** aggregate D4 risk missed by individual assessment; assess component and combined dependency (KCS topology).
72. **Providers coordinate withdrawal to force compliance:** distributed ownership does not immunise coordinated capture.

# PART U — TEMPORAL CHANGE

73. **Ordinary D1 service evolves into D4:** dependency classification must be dynamic; knowledge and notification affect responsibility.
74. **Critical service becomes ordinary after redundancy:** enhanced duty should contract with diminished dependency.
75. **Participant secretly removes redundancy:** self-created fragility affects cost/responsibility without erasing standing.

# 86. Cross-test result

The architecture survives.

No case requires either:

**Owner Controls Asset -> Owner Controls Participant**

or:

**Participant Depends On Asset -> Participant Owns Asset**

The difficult cases instead require a third category:

> **bounded dependency-sensitive transition and reconciliation.**

This validates the central architecture.

# 87. Refinement 1 — adequate successor

A successor is adequate only if it preserves the material function that created the protected dependency.

Therefore:

**Available Alternative != Adequate Successor**

and:

**Nominally Equivalent Service != Functionally Equivalent Transition**

Relevant dimensions can include:

- life/health;
- cognition;
- continuity;
- accessibility;
- privacy;
- security;
- protected information;
- identity;
- agency.

---

# 88. Refinement 2 — transition opportunity versus acceptance

Provider duty cannot depend forever on participant agreement.

Once an adequate successor and reasonable transition opportunity exist:

**Participant Refusal != Automatic Extension Of Provider Duty**

subject to review of competence, adequacy and coercion.

---

# 89. Refinement 3 — transition duty is dynamic

Dependency can rise or fall.

Therefore:

**Initial Dependency Class != Permanent Dependency Class**

and:

**Past Critical Dependency != Permanent Enhanced Provider Duty**

The PSI relation requires reassessment at consequential change.

---

# 90. Refinement 4 — aggregate dependency

Distributed infrastructure creates a new ESCP risk.

Each provider may appear individually non-critical while simultaneous withdrawal is catastrophic.

Therefore:

> **Dependency must be assessed at both component and aggregate functional levels.**

**Individually Non-Critical Withdrawal Can Be Collectively Critical**

This should integrate with KCS dependency topology.

---

# 91. Refinement 5 — participant-manufactured dependency

Anti-capture must be symmetric.

**Manufactured Dependency By Participant != Automatic Expansion Of Provider Duty**

**Self-Created Fragility != Automatic Transfer Of Responsibility**

This does not erase standing or permit gratuitous catastrophic harm.

It affects responsibility, cost and transition burden.

# 92. Refinement 6 — controller-created alternatives deficit

Where the controller deliberately destroys or blocks alternatives:

**Controller-Created Absence Of Alternatives != Neutral Market Condition**

This strengthens the duty to repair or enable exit.

# 93. Refinement 7 — criticality and contract

Contract matters but does not exhaust the rights analysis.

**No-Guarantee Contract != Advance Authority For Avoidable Catastrophic Harm In Every Future State**

**Critical Dependency != Automatic Erasure Of Contractual Obligations**

# 94. Refinement 8 — three-party resource solution

Many hard cases reveal an incomplete binary:

**Provider Pays**

versus:

**Dependent Participant Loses Function**

ESCP requires asking whether a third route exists:

- insurer;
- public continuity reserve;
- mutual pool;
- emergency fund;
- replacement provider;
- creditor restructuring;
- donated/shared resources;
- temporary civil infrastructure.

Therefore:

**Binary Owner-Or-Dependent Cost Allocation Can Be An Incomplete Evaluation Space**

# 95. Refinement 9 — emergency preservation without continued operation

Some dangerous participants/systems must stop operating immediately.

Standing can still be respected through:

- safe isolation;
- state preservation;
- later recovery;
- review;
- migration where possible.

Therefore:

**Continuity Protection != Continuous Operation**

and:

**Safe State Can Preserve Standing Without Preserving Active Capability**

---

# 96. Refinement 10 — transition feasibility

The phrase “where reasonably possible” needs explicit structure.

Feasibility should consider:

- physical possibility;
- time;
- available resources;
- risk to others;
- technical maturity;
- provider capacity;
- participant capacity;
- substitute availability;
- reversibility;
- expected harm.

**Transition Duty Must Remain Feasibility-Bounded**

But feasibility must not be manipulated by deliberately removing transition capacity.

---

# 97. Refinement 11 — preservation versus service

Where active service cannot continue, preservation may still be possible.

Examples:

- AI state quarantine;
- medical stabilisation;
- protected storage;
- inactive embodiment;
- emergency archive pending migration.

Therefore:

**Service Continuation != State Preservation**

and:

**Inability To Continue Service != Automatic Authority To Destroy Preservable Participant State**

This links directly to CPS and Continuity.

---

# 98. Refinement 12 — transition debt

A provider may complete urgent safe transition while legitimate unpaid costs remain.

This avoids the false binary between catastrophic withdrawal and uncompensated provision.

Candidate concept:

> **Transition Debt** — a legitimate unresolved cost/obligation created or preserved while immediate continuity protection is performed.

This is not yet a legal debt class.

It is a design concept for Economy/Law.

**Emergency Transition != Automatic Cost Waiver**

# 99. Major unresolved interfaces

The test exposes six major interfaces.

### I. Scarcity allocation

How should genuinely insufficient indivisible resources be allocated among equal-standing participants?

### II. Bankruptcy / creditor priority

How should participant continuity interact with secured claims, insolvency and liquidation?

### III. Identity-preserving migration

What constitutes adequate successor service where migration may copy, branch, suspend or transform a participant?

### IV. Shared cognition and third-party information

How can portability preserve one participant without appropriating another participant's cognition or protected information?

### V. Permanently inseparable private infrastructure

What legal structure applies where participant and privately owned asset cannot be separated?

### VI. Cost allocation / transition debt

Who ultimately bears transition costs after urgent continuity protection?

None defeats PSI itself.

---

# 100. Overall finding

**PASS WITH TWELVE ARCHITECTURAL REFINEMENTS AND SIX MAJOR DOWNSTREAM INTERFACES.**

The architecture survives both primary attacks.

It does not require expropriating supporting infrastructure merely because dependency exists.

It does not permit infrastructure ownership to become ownership or arbitrary destruction of the participant.

The central relation is strengthened:

> **Severe dependency can create a bounded duty of safe separation without creating permanent ownership in either direction.**

This can be represented as:

**Legitimate Property/Service Relation**
→ **Material Participant Dependency**
→ **Dependency-Sensitive Transition Duty**
→ **Adequate Successor / Preservation / Safe Separation**
→ **Provider Exit + Participant Continuity Where Feasible**
→ **Residual Cost/Obligation/Remedy Resolution**

The strongest new finding is:

> **The civil objective is not indefinite provision. It is safe separation where separation is possible.**

And where separation is not presently possible:

> **Dependency must be reconciled explicitly rather than disguised as either absolute property power or automatic participant ownership of the resource.**
