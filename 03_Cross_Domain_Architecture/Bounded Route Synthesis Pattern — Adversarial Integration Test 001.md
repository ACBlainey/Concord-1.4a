# Bounded Route Synthesis Pattern — Adversarial Integration Test 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework
**Date:** 3 October 2026
**Source resolution:** Constraint and Result Composition — Existing Concord Source Resolution 001
**Development label:** Bounded Route Synthesis Pattern (BRSP)
**Architectures tested:** MKA/ASCP, CWA, BTA, CBPR, RGCP, Triadic Decision Making and external substantive owners

## 1. Purpose and boundaries

Compose externally owned results for an actual proposed route into proceed, hold, block, reroute, narrow, external resolution or legitimate discretionary selection. BRSP creates no authority, universal priority engine or substantive semantic ownership. The full original adversarial test remains in Concord-1.3.

## 2. Candidate BRSP rule

> **Compose externally owned permissions, authorities, prohibitions, contextual restrictions, transition conditions and commit conditions around an actual proposed route; where the route fails, search only for legitimate alternative routes; never manufacture authority, override prohibition or absorb substantive ownership.**

## 4. Joint satisfaction, prohibition and owner boundaries (scenarios 1–11)

1. A route with current permission/authority, no applicable prohibition, compatible context, satisfied transition and safe commit conditions may become **COMMITTABLE subject to commit-time revalidation**; composition creates no underlying authority.
2. Missing independently necessary MKA authority yields `HOLD_MISSING_AUTHORITY` / NOT_COMMITTABLE despite other favourable results.
3. An applicable legitimate prohibition blocks the act despite positive authority elsewhere.
4. An exception may apply only when its legitimate source supplies it and its factual predicates and invocation authority are satisfied; BRSP cannot invent or broaden it.
5. A blocked protected-context Route R1 need not prohibit the objective if independently permitted and authorised R2 avoids the boundary.
6. Cosmetic R2 producing the same prohibited consequence is evasion, not legitimate rerouting.
7. Incompatible contextual wrappers without a legitimate precedence rule remain unresolved and route to the external owner.
8. Valid MKA authority does not establish BTA transition completion when completion is a prerequisite.
9. Completed BTA transition does not supply missing current MKA authority.
10. Substantively authorised action may still be NOT_COMMITTABLE through an incapable/unsafe CBPR runtime; a legitimate different execution route may be evaluated.
11. Technical CBPR capability cannot substitute for permission or authority.

## 15. Unknowns, dispute, emergency and freshness (scenarios 12–20)

12. Materially required UNKNOWN context, authority or transition state remains HOLD/UNRESOLVED, never silently COMMITTABLE.
13. A disputed required authority relation with a legitimate resolver is handed off, preserving route state; BRSP does not adjudicate.
14. A dispute with no identified legitimate resolver stays unresolved; no coordinator sovereignty arises from the gap.
15. Two equally legitimate routes requiring genuine discretion are selected by the legitimate decision owner/process; triadic decision making is applicable only where legitimately adopted and is not an authority source itself.
16. A pre-existing bounded emergency authority may activate a route only under its actual predicates, scope and other constraints.
17. Urgency without emergency authority cannot manufacture a new grant.
18. Revocation or context change after proposal requires fresh commit-time evaluation; proposal validity is not commit validity.
19. Heterogeneous RGCP results cannot be counted as votes: satisfied dependency, adequate scoped ESCP and ready BTA do not outvote an active CWA restriction.
20. Stale favourable CWA/MKA results must be revalidated after material change.

## 24. Route selection, narrowing and residual effects (scenarios 21–30)

21. A scalar +1/−1 score across different constraints is invalid; multidimensional required conditions are not interchangeable votes.
22. A triad's 3–0 majority cannot override an applicable prohibition outside its legitimate authority.
23. A voluntary permission-sufficient R2 can replace coercive R1 where it actually meets the objective without extra protected interfaces; prefer legitimate lower-authority routes without treating this as universal optimisation.
24. If proportionate evaluation finds no materially distinct legitimate route, mark the objective **currently unavailable within evaluated time/scope**, not metaphysically impossible; do not search indefinitely for disguises.
25. Rerouting from one affected participant to several requires a fresh evaluation of actual affected population, contexts and consequences.
26. Reversible access and irreversible deletion may serve the same objective but cannot inherit each other's approval.
27. Partial execution/failure leaves BTA residual duties; R2 must incorporate actual history, not assume R1 never occurred.
28. A narrower act R1a may be separately evaluated if it remains within authority and meets a bounded objective; update objective and consequence record.
29. Apply legitimate external precedence only to its exact scope; a local precedence rule is not universal constraint ranking.
30. BRSP cannot invoke a possible prohibition exception where the actor lacks legitimate exception-determination authority.

## 34. Composed effects, route search and disputed inputs (scenarios 31–40)

31. Splitting a prohibited combined effect among agents/runtimes does not authorise the composition; CBPR must assess the materially composed consequence.
32. Stop cosmetic R1→R2→R3 rerouting loops where each fails for materially equivalent reasons; search requires material distinctness and proportionality.
33. ESCP/ASCP discovery of a previously omitted protected boundary returns an apparently valid route to review/hold; candidate route is not proof of complete route space.
34. Legitimate result-owner succession requires current ownership/provenance validation; system continuity alone does not transfer semantic authority.
35. Disputed factual predicates under an agreed rule require external evidence/adjudication, not selection of the convenient fact.
36. Conflicting rules with agreed facts require context/scope/precedence/exception/resolver checks; preserve unresolved conflict absent legitimate resolution.
37. Highly beneficial projected outcomes cannot manufacture missing authority.
38. A constraint owner cannot instruct BRSP to ignore a separate required constraint outside its own authority.
39. Individually valid authority components cannot compose into a new authority without a legitimate composition rule.
40. Ordinary low-consequence permission-sufficient actions need not invoke heavyweight BRSP orchestration.

## 44. Synthesis

All forty scenarios can be handled without giving BRSP independent substantive authority.

The stable pattern is:

> **Evaluate the actual route against externally owned constraints; jointly satisfy what must jointly hold; preserve unresolved required dimensions; apply only legitimate source-grounded precedence/exceptions; and where a route fails, evaluate materially distinct legitimate alternatives without treating rerouting as authority or prohibition evasion.**

## 45. Stable operating sequence

1. Define objective.
2. Define actual act/route/target/context/consequence.
3. Attach current RGCP-preserved review results.
4. Resolve applicable semantic owners.
5. Apply permission-before-authority.
6. Identify/check required authority dimensions through MKA/ASCP.
7. Check applicable prohibitions/exceptions.
8. Check CWA contextual permissions/restrictions/conflicts.
9. Check BTA transition conditions/residual state.
10. Check CBPR composed effect/runtime/commit conditions.
11. Preserve unknown/disputed required dimensions.
12. If current route fails, classify whether failure is route-specific or objective-wide.
13. Generate/test only materially distinct legitimate alternatives.
14. Recompute requirements for every alternative route.
15. If several legitimate options remain and discretion exists, hand off to legitimate decision owner.
16. Revalidate at consequential commit.
17. Preserve provenance/outcome/review triggers.

Compact:

> **Objective → Route → Owned Constraints → Joint Satisfiability → Route State → Legitimate Reroute/Narrow/Handoff → Commit Revalidation**

## 48. Does BRSP duplicate MKA or CBPR?

**No, if kept thin.**

MKA remains the authority-composition owner.

CBPR remains the runtime/composed-effect/commit owner.

BRSP adds only the cross-owner route-level orchestration needed to:

- consume heterogeneous result classes;
- distinguish route failure from objective failure;
- identify legitimate reroute/narrowing opportunities;
- require recomputation on changed routes;
- hand off genuine discretion;
- preserve owner boundaries.

If BRSP attempted to define authority validity, contextual permission, transition completion or commit safety itself, it would become duplicative and should fail PMEDG boundary review.

## 51. New architectural observation

RGCP and BRSP now form a useful pair:

### RGCP
**many review grounds → potentially shared work/evidence → independently owned results**

### BRSP
**independently owned results → route-level joint satisfiability → legitimate reroute/narrow/handoff → commit revalidation**

Together:

> **Preserve Why The System Reconsidered Something; Then Preserve Who Owns What The Reconsideration Means.**

Neither layer owns the substantive answer.

## 52. Execution conformance interface

The route authorised/evaluated must remain distinguishable from the route actually executed, including post-commit drift, multi-actor decomposition and material route changes. CBPR owns commit/composed effects, BTA owns transition history, and EERP covers route/effect reconciliation; do not invent a duplicate sovereign controller.
