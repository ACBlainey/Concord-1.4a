# PART A — CONSENT AND INITIATION

1. **Unconscious A:** consent cannot be sole authority source; emergency protective treatment and legitimate prospective resource governance may independently apply. **Consent absence != resource-governance absence.**
2. **Conscious A not informed:** independent public mandate may survive lack of notice, but concealment weakens procedural legitimacy and may be institutional failure. **Independent authority != permission to conceal material conditions.**
3. **A rejects scarcity condition:** refusal may affect consent to treatment, but does not automatically turn a lawfully pooled resource into permanent individual property.
4. **A agrees only because no alternative exists:** structurally constrained acceptance cannot waive constitutional standing; resource rule must independently stand.

# PART B — RULE TIMING

5. **Rule precedes treatment:** strongest prospective case; still subject to legitimacy, scope and current predicates.
6. **Rule created after treatment begins:** cannot automatically create retrospective withdrawal authority; reliance and protected support context require independent transition analysis.
7. **Rule materially changed later:** original authority does not cover materially different future terms without reassessment.
8. **Dynamic scarcity rule:** prospectively bounded objective triggers may be legitimate; undefined future administrator discretion is not.

# PART C — ABUNDANCE BECOMES SCARCITY

9. **Abundance becomes scarcity:** later genuine disaster can activate valid prospective resource governance but cannot alone create established-support withdrawal authority.
10. **Foreseeable scarcity, no written rule:** failure to plan is not authority; independently valid emergency rules may still apply.

# PART D — MISSING KEY RECREATES FIRST-COME

11. **No incumbent withdrawal key:** practically permanent first allocations create de facto first-come outcomes, not greater worth. Prospectively define the withdrawal status of foreseeably scarce pooled systems; never invent authority bedside.
12. **Deliberately omitted key:** intentional omission favouring incumbents is a policy choice, not neutral architecture.

# PART E — POOLED RESOURCE LAUNDERING

13. **Retrospective pooled label:** administrative relabelling cannot create pooling authority.
14. **Public device permanently implanted in A:** title does not confer bodily/invasive recovery authority; functional integration changes the intervention.
15. **A owns device:** scarcity alone cannot requisition private property; independent authority and remedy may be required.
16. **Privately lent device:** pooling terms and law govern; temporary lending is not permanent expropriation.

# PART F — PARTICIPANT MATERIAL

17. **A's organ:** scarcity allocation does not authorise harvesting a participant; body is not an ordinary pooled resource.
18. **Inseparable device recovery kills A:** this is direct participant destruction, not ordinary reallocation; resource authority is insufficient.

# PART G — DIRECT INTERVENTION

19. **Disconnect without touching A:** withdrawal authority remains necessary despite minimal bodily contact.
20. **Surgical removal:** distinct direct-intervention authority required.
21. **Severe avoidable withdrawal pain:** withdrawal authority does not authorise unnecessary added harm; residual care matters.

# PART H — FALSE EMERGENCY

22. **Exaggerated scarcity:** false predicate invalidates emergency key.
23. **Budget cut:** financial scarcity does not automatically satisfy emergency legal criteria; cause, alternatives and provenance matter.
24. **Deliberate underinvestment:** manufactured scarcity creates accountability questions and can constrain claimed emergency authority.

# PART I — EMERGENCY DURATION

25. **Two-hour emergency:** rapid bounded procedures may be appropriate.
26. **Three-year emergency:** sunset and reassessment required; chronic scarcity must transition toward ordinary prospective law, not perpetual emergency power.

# PART J — TIME PRESSURE

27. **Keys verifiable in 60 seconds:** proceed only if current keys and predicates are valid.
28. **Thirty seconds, withdrawal key unverified:** urgency cannot convert unknown authority into verified authority; precomputed authoritative state enables rapid checks without inventing keys.
29. **Authority register offline:** resilient signed local/offline records and legitimate fallback are needed; infrastructure failure does not expand authority.

# PART K — KEY HOLDERS

30. **Same clinician holds all keys:** multiple formal fields do not create independent authority functions.
31. **Three committees under one allocator:** nominal organisational separation is not functional independence.
32. **Allocator-owned reviewing algorithm:** automation cannot self-certify independence or authority.

# PART L — COLLUSION

33. **Falsified prognosis:** formally valid-looking keys fail when predicates are falsified; independent evidentiary review and provenance needed.
34. **All institutions collude:** no formal architecture guarantees against total capture; recursive oversight, external contestability, audit and Historical provenance remain necessary.

# PART M — AGGREGATION RATIOS

35. **One versus two comparable participants:** greater aggregate consequence may matter, but the materiality threshold is substantive, not automatic authority.
36. **One versus 100:** stronger aggregate reason still does not create withdrawal authority.
37. **One versus one:** no count advantage; legitimate residual tie-break may be needed.

# PART N — PROGNOSIS

38. **A 90%, B–F 20% each:** expected outcomes may matter without reducing participant worth to fractional values.
39. **Highly uncertain prognosis:** confidence/materiality architecture required; model precision is not clinical certainty.
40. **50.1% versus 50.0%:** pseudo-precision cannot substitute for a material difference.

# PART O — INCOMPARABLE HARMS

41. **Death versus temporary disability:** count does not establish harm equivalence.

# PART P — DEVELOPMENTAL PARTICIPANT

# PART X — MALICIOUS SCARCITY CREATION

# PART Y — TRANSITION IMPOSSIBLE

65. **Withdrawal inevitably kills A despite best feasible care:** unavoidable harm does not create authority, but also does not automatically negate separately legitimate keys; distinguish authority from feasibility and consequence.
66. **Withdrawal kills A and ruins resource for B–F:** allocation effectiveness fails; no reallocation benefit.

67. **Surgically killing A to recover R:** mechanism changes ordinary withdrawal into instrumentalisation/direct killing; pooled-resource authority alone is insufficient.
68. **Disconnection leads to death from underlying condition:** still grave withdrawal, but distinct from killing as the means of extraction; requires its own valid authority.

# PART AA — CONSTITUTIONAL PROHIBITION

69. **Emergency rule conflicts with higher prohibition:** higher rule blocks the proposed direct intervention.
70. **Five lives invoked to override prohibition:** aggregation arithmetic cannot manufacture a constitutional exception.

# PART AB — SUBSTITUTE APPEARS

71. **Substitute arrives before withdrawal:** scarcity changes; revalidate the authority stack at execution.
72. **Substitute might arrive minutes later:** evidence, timing and consequence matter; no universal allocation answer.

# PART AC — INVALIDITY DISCOVERED AFTER ACTION

73. **Scarcity data later proven false:** physical action cannot be undone; preserve audit, remedy, accountability and system correction.
74. **Reasonable evidence-based error:** bad outcome is not automatic misconduct; remedy may still be required.

# PART AD — RAPID AUTHORITY VERIFICATION

76. **Locally available signed authority bundle:** precompute law/rule version, emergency/resource class, withdrawal/intervention class; verify current factual predicates quickly.
77. **Precomputed patient outcomes:** impermissible substitute for live clinical/contextual facts. **Precomputed authority != precomputed outcome.**
78. **AI verifies keys:** automation may assist verification but cannot become authority source.

# PART AE — STALE KEYS

79. **Clinical validity changed five minutes ago:** stale key fails; authority completeness includes temporal freshness.
80. **Scarcity ended but database lags:** cached scarcity is not current scarcity; use best available evidence and freshness checks.

# PART AF — CONDITIONAL RESOURCE STATUS

81. **Private R becomes lawfully pooled in emergency:** resource classification is temporal, not permanent identity.
82. **Emergency ends while R in use:** pooling authority contracts/sunsets under law, not permanent expropriation.

# PART AG — REPEATED REALLOCATION

83. **A displaced for B–F, then G–Z arrive:** avoid unbounded churn unless prospective rule authorises dynamic reallocation and accounts for transition harm.
84. **Repeated switching reduces survival:** transition cost is part of allocation consequence, not frictionless reallocation.

# PART AH — SYSTEMATIC MINORITY LOSS

85. **AI participants repeatedly lose numeric comparisons:** equal standing requires longitudinal fairness audit; repeated aggregate defeat does not imply lesser worth.
86. **Rare biological minority similarly loses:** same longitudinal exclusion concern.

# PART AI — RESOURCE EFFICIENCY

87. **A needs ten units, ten others need one each:** efficiency can affect consequence without measuring worth; withdrawal still needs authority.
88. **A's higher need arises from disability:** disability cannot become a worth penalty; actual resource consequences remain subject to legitimate criteria.

# PART AK — FALSIFICATION SYNTHESIS

## 91–96. Tested failure modes

1. **First-come priority:** missing established-support withdrawal authority can create de facto incumbency without establishing greater participant worth. Foreseeably scarce pooled systems should define withdrawal status prospectively; bedside urgency cannot invent a missing key.
2. **Prospective-rule laundering:** a pooled label is not authority, conditional resource use is not conditional participant standing, a later rule does not automatically apply retrospectively, and public title does not authorise bodily recovery.
3. **Emergency speed:** precompute rule/authority state and verify live clinical, scarcity, intervention and route predicates at execution. Do not precompute the final patient decision.
4. **Public monopoly:** public control strengthens stewardship duties; it does not establish ownership over participants.
5. **Functional independence:** multiple keys or committees are not independent when one actor controls their effective source. Verify functional separation, conflicts and evidence provenance.
6. **Overall synthesis:** the architecture preserves protected established support without granting permanent resource sovereignty and permits legitimate reallocation only through current, independently grounded keys. Genuinely absent authority or unresolved comparability remains unresolved rather than being invented.

# 100. Rapid mode

For genuine time-critical cases:

### Precompute

- valid law/rule version;
- resource classification;
- authority holders;
- emergency triggers;
- withdrawal classes;
- direct-intervention classes;
- prohibited criteria;
- required review level;
- sunset conditions.

### Runtime verify

- participant/support identity;
- current clinical function;
- current scarcity;
- current alternatives;
- current allocation consequence;
- current trigger satisfaction;
- current authority validity;
- current intervention mechanism.

### Execute

only within validated authority.

### Afterwards

- audit;
- remedy where applicable;
- outcome learning;
- rule correction.

This gives speed without collapsing authority boundaries.

# 101. Strongest finding

The test supports:

> **Authority should be prepared before the emergency, but factual predicates must remain current at the moment of action.**

and:

> **A scarce-resource system may govern the resource without owning the participant, and an established support relation may constrain withdrawal without automatically transferring permanent control of the resource to the participant.**

This is the reconciliation the earlier architectures were missing.
