# ESCP Completeness Challenge — Bounded Discovery and Termination Test 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Date:** 3 October 2026  
**Status:** CROSS-ARCHITECTURE ADVERSARIAL TEST / COMPLETE / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL  
**Primary source:** Evaluation-Space Completeness Problem — A Portable Model of Correct Evaluation Within an Incomplete Representational Space  
**Predecessor:** ESCP Across Representational Architectures — Completeness Without Centralisation 001

## 1. Purpose

ESCP bounded discovery must avoid premature closure and infinite completeness review. The operational goal is **COMPLETE_WITHIN_DECLARED_SCOPE** with a consequence-proportionate stopping condition, never a claim of absolute completeness. The full original test remains in Concord-1.3.

## 2. Core problem

**Being right about everything measured does not prove that everything material was measured.** But unspecified possible omissions alone cannot prevent every decision. Bounded discovery asks when enough proportionate, material completeness-directed search supports action within a declared scope, without claiming exhaustive reality knowledge.

## 3. Candidate bounded-discovery model

Represent a completeness challenge as:

`C = <Representation, IntendedConclusion, DeclaredScope, Consequence, Irreversibility, KnownMissing, MaterialUnknowns, DiscoveryRoutes, DiscoveryYield, ResidualUncertainty, ReviewHorizon, Provenance>`

Discovery continues where a candidate omission is:

- materially relevant;
- reasonably plausible;
- discoverable at proportionate cost/time;
- capable of changing the conclusion, route, authority or consequence.

Discovery may terminate where:

- known material gaps are resolved or explicitly preserved;
- no unresolved plausible omission is currently capable of materially changing the decision within scope;
- available discovery routes have been reasonably exercised relative to consequence;
- further search has low expected decision relevance;
- residual uncertainty is explicitly represented;
- reversibility/review mechanisms are proportionate to what remains unknown.

## 4. Termination rule

Candidate rule:

> **An ESCP completeness challenge may terminate as COMPLETE_WITHIN_DECLARED_SCOPE when known material gaps have been resolved or preserved as explicit uncertainty, reasonably discoverable material omission pathways have been proportionately tested, no currently identified unresolved omission is sufficient to invalidate the scoped conclusion, and residual uncertainty is matched by appropriate reversibility, monitoring or review.**

This is not:

`RealityComplete`

It is:

`EvaluationSufficientForDeclaredScopeAtTimeT`

## 5. Material gaps, discovery yield and observer diversity (cases 1–10)

1. Known relevant unmeasured X: retain `KNOWN_MISSING_DIMENSION`; accurate other variables do not establish completeness.
2. Missing X shown immaterial by source-grounded analysis: scoped termination remains possible; missing does not imply materially missing.
3. Observer B has a sensor/interface A lacks and disagrees: credible alternative material distinction warrants proportionate discovery, not automatic acceptance of B.
4. Only speculative “something unimaginable” without mechanism, anomaly, relation, evidence or discovery route: no indefinite veto; preserve epistemic humility.
5. Low-cost reversible configuration with weak plausible gap: lower predecision burden, retain provenance and correction/review.
6. Irreversible elimination of independent evidence with plausible missing dimension: higher completeness burden, prefer additional discovery/preservation or less irreversible route where reasonably possible.
7. Independent searches yield only known, immaterial or no new material dimensions: diminishing yield supports scoped termination, not proof of exhaustiveness.
8. Searches continue finding conclusion-changing dimensions: arbitrary count limit does not justify declaring completeness.
9. Heterogeneous observers using genuinely different models/interfaces converge: stronger scoped confidence, not completeness proof.
10. Thousand homogeneous observers share sensors/categories/training: agreement within their shared space does not establish dimensional coverage.

## 15. Discovery boundaries, missing relationships and scope adjustment (cases 11–20)

11. Only invasive/destructive discovery route: ESCP does not confer permission or authority to use it; MKA/CWA and domain owners retain control.
12. Time-critical decision: prioritise high-value discovery within horizon, record incompleteness, prefer least unnecessarily irreversible adequate route and preserve review/recovery; limited time neither proves completeness nor mandates inaction.
13. Meta-challenge of ESCP itself: apply scoped proportionality recursively where warranted, not infinite regress.
14. Repeated unspecified “unknown unknown” objection without new material mechanism/evidence: record but do not grant permanent veto.
15. Model-inconvenient anomaly plausibly material to conclusion: investigate, bound or demonstrate immateriality before termination.
16. Accurate object measurements but omitted material relation: completeness includes relationships, not only attributes.
17. Present model adequate but conclusion predicts unrepresented future state: expand temporal evaluation or narrow conclusion.
18. Narrow claim (“A outperforms B on measured tasks”) may be justified where broad claim (“B is globally redundant”) is not; scope narrowing can resolve completeness mismatch.
19. Reversible route preserving future evidence may reduce required completeness relative to an irreversible alternative, without being universally superior.
20. Recognised material unknown cannot currently be discovered: do not infer absence; narrow claim, select reversible route, defer, act under explicit uncertainty if necessary, monitor/review and preserve evidence as appropriate.

## 25. Dispute, reopening, proportionality and high-stakes safeguards (cases 21–30)

21. Competent evaluators dispute adequacy: `DISPUTED_COMPLETENESS`; seek heterogeneous review, source resolution or legitimate bounded procedure; ESCP cannot adjudicate itself.
22. A newly available high-value observer/interface can reopen review despite prior diminishing discovery yield.
23. Postdecision discovery of a new material dimension: preserve original bounded evaluation, later evidence and correction; later discovery alone does not prove earlier bad faith or negligence.
24. Very low-consequence decision with exorbitant discovery route: proportionate termination need not exhaust available search at any cost.
25. Highly irreversible decision with cheap, credible, rapid material discovery unused: termination is unjustified.
26. “Just in case” private-data search: ESCP creates no surveillance, consent or protected-space override authority.
27. Completeness score optimised while missing dimensions outside metric: score is evidence, not completeness proof.
28. Exhausted checklist of known omission types is useful discipline but cannot prove absence of unlisted omission classes.
29. Low-consequence, highly reversible, well-tested decision with no known gap/anomaly and proportionate heterogeneous checks: `COMPLETE_WITHIN_DECLARED_SCOPE` is justified.
30. Existentially irreversible elimination of heterogeneous evaluator/population: no known gap alone is insufficient for the strongest completeness claim; use stronger preservation/discovery safeguards where reasonably possible, without an absolute prohibition.

## 35. Bounded discovery ladder

A practical ESCP challenge can proceed through:

### Level 0 — Scope alignment
What exactly is the conclusion?

### Level 1 — Known gaps
What relevant dimensions are known to be missing?

### Level 2 — Internal anomalies
What does the current representation fail to explain?

### Level 3 — Interface alternatives
What other sensors, observers, models or routes can expose distinctions the current architecture cannot?

### Level 4 — Relationship/state expansion
Are material relationships, temporal states, failure modes or future states absent?

### Level 5 — Adversarial challenge
What would have to be true for the scoped conclusion to fail?

### Level 6 — Consequence test
How irreversible/broad is the decision, and what evidence/reversibility should that require?

The ladder is not mandatory ceremony for every decision.

Depth is consequence-proportionate.

## 36. Termination dimensions

A bounded termination decision should consider:

1. **Scope clarity** — is the intended conclusion precisely bounded?
2. **Known gaps** — are material known gaps resolved or explicitly represented?
3. **Anomalies** — are material anomalies investigated or preserved?
4. **Discovery routes** — have proportionate high-value routes been exercised?
5. **Observer/interface diversity** — where useful, has independent representation been sampled?
6. **Discovery yield** — is further search still finding material dimensions?
7. **Consequence** — what happens if the evaluation is incomplete?
8. **Irreversibility** — can error be corrected?
9. **Time horizon** — how much discovery is possible before decision?
10. **Residual uncertainty** — is it explicit?
11. **Review/recovery** — can later discoveries reopen the conclusion?
12. **Authority** — are discovery methods themselves legitimately permitted?

## 37. Termination states

Suggested portable states:

- `COMPLETE_WITHIN_DECLARED_SCOPE`
- `KNOWN_MATERIAL_GAP`
- `MATERIAL_UNKNOWN`
- `DISPUTED_COMPLETENESS`
- `DISCOVERY_REQUIRED`
- `DISCOVERY_OPTIONAL`
- `CONCLUSION_SCOPE_TOO_BROAD`
- `TIME_BOUNDED_INCOMPLETE`
- `REVIEW_DUE`

No state means absolutely complete.

## 38. Anti-paralysis rule

> **ESCP review should continue while a reasonably plausible, materially relevant omission pathway remains unresolved and further discovery is proportionate to the decision.**

It may stop when that condition no longer holds within the declared scope and horizon.

## 39. Anti-closure rule

> **Termination means sufficient evaluation for the declared decision scope at the current time; it does not mean reality has been proven exhaustively represented.**

## 40. Reopening rule

A terminated completeness review reopens when materially relevant new information appears, including:

- anomaly;
- new observer/interface;
- new dependency;
- changed context;
- changed route;
- changed consequence;
- changed time horizon;
- new relationship;
- new failure mode;
- new substantive evidence.

KCS/STRA may operationalise this reopening.

## 41. Relationship to KCS and STRA

KCS can propagate the discovery of a newly established material dimension to represented dependents.

STRA can trigger/re-route completeness review when declared conditions arise.

Neither decides the substantive meaning of the missing dimension.

## 42. Relationship to MKA/ASCP

For authority questions, ASCP uses the same bounded logic.

A high-consequence act demands stronger authority-space completeness than a low-consequence reversible act.

But:

> **Need For Greater Authority Completeness != Authority To Invent Missing Authority**

## 43. Relationship to CWA

Contextual completeness review cannot justify invasive access merely to eliminate every possible unknown.

Context discovery remains bounded by permission, rights and authority.

## 44. Relationship to BTA

Transition completeness review should scale with consequence and irreversibility.

A reversible low-impact handoff may require less discovery than a permanent multi-owner state transformation.

BTA still owns transition completion.

## 47. Operational synthesis

**Complete Within Declared Scope != Complete Reality. Bounded Termination != Epistemic Closure.** Sufficient evaluation is a scoped, consequence- and time-indexed claim, with material gaps represented, proportionate discovery performed, residual uncertainty explicit and correction/reopening preserved where reasonably possible.
