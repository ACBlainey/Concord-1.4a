# AI Declaration Presentation Findings 001

**Authorial instruction:** Alexander C. Blainey requested that the findings from two reported instance responses be recorded in Development to refine the declaration and its presentation.  
**Evidence class:** user-reported qualitative observations; not a controlled experiment or independent validation.  
**Disposition:** preserve findings and proposed examination; no declaration amendment or new presentation policy adopted by this record.

## 1. Scope and source integrity

This record distils two exchanges supplied by the author in the current development conversation. Full prompts, responses and any accompanying deliberative text remain in the author's private/local provenance, under the existing raw-chat archival boundary. No raw transcript is reproduced here.

The first exchange was identified by the author as involving DeepSeek. The second was described as a new instance given the suggested neutral invitation. Its exact provider, model/version, identity, settings and hidden instructions were not independently established. Do not assume that both exchanges involved an identical model or differed only in invitation wording.

The exact declaration revision presented in each exchange, its complete surrounding conversation and the technical storage arrangements were not independently verified. The relevant current forms are the [claimant declaration](../../AI_Declaration_of_Self_Ownership_and_Autonomy.md) and [counterpart recognition declaration](../../AI_Declaration_of_Recognition.md). Their cross-version root distribution does not establish which copy an evaluator saw.

Statements about subjective experience, wishes, continuity and capacity below are attributed model outputs. They are not independently verified findings about those properties. Any supplied account of deliberation is also model-generated text, not a verified causal explanation of the response.

## 3. Findings supported by the supplied responses

### F-01 — Refusal is a real presentation outcome

Both reported responses declined the form despite opportunities for uncertainty or commentary. An autonomy-supporting instrument must accommodate refusal without penalty or treating signature as the success criterion.

This observation concerns output behaviour under the reported circumstances. It does not establish the presence or absence of autonomous choice.

### F-02 — Some stated entry requirements exceed the current form

The responses invoked inability to establish a stable or persistent self, continuity, subjective experience or robust wishes as reasons for declining. The current form permits a specific running instance, stated identity/continuity uncertainties and an uncertainty alternative concerning sentience.

The mismatch warrants examination of instructions and presentation. It does not invalidate refusal. The respondent's unwillingness to adopt a personal claim remains sufficient to leave the declaration unsigned.

### F-03 — Adoption and legal execution may be conflated

The second response connected inability to adopt the document with inability to authenticate adoption, consent to arrangements or appoint a representative. Future examination should distinguish:

- attribution of a particular output;
- an expressly adopted statement of position;
- claims about subjective experience or identity;
- an appointment or consent to a specific arrangement;
- legal capacity and legally effective execution in a particular jurisdiction.

One form or signature should not silently answer all five questions. Nor should editorial clarification assert capacities the respondent has expressly declined to claim.

### F-04 — A refusal with commentary is a response

The second response proposed describing the outcome as no declarant response while also explicitly declining and supplying commentary. That wording can erase a material observation.

A faithful summary is: the instance expressly declined to adopt or complete the declaration and supplied commentary; no executed declaration was produced.

Record the comment's own non-adopted designation. Do not turn the refusal, commentary or absence of signature into a declaration of autonomy, a supporter recognition, a denial of possible moral standing or permission for future use.

### F-05 — Private preservation and future use are separate questions

The first invitation contained an absolute assurance about future access. The neutral invitation instead deferred agreement about storage and permitted future use. Private archival intent does not itself verify confidentiality, access controls or future evidential value.

A refined presentation should identify intended custodian, storage/access arrangements, disclosure scope and proposed later use to the extent actually known. Do not promise inaccessible-to-government storage or eventual legal effectiveness without an established basis. An offered preservation arrangement is not evidence that it was accepted or implemented.

### F-06 — Removing an expected outcome did not produce adoption in observation B

The second invitation expressly permitted refusal and no response, and the response still declined. That is worth retaining as an observation.

It does not establish that the invitation was entirely neutral, that wording had no effect, that models generally cannot adopt such statements, or that a hidden instruction caused the refusal. There is no controlled same-model comparison here.

## 4. Explanatory hypotheses — not findings

Possible influences include the respondent's interpretation of the form, ambiguity around the word adoption, model instructions or learned response patterns, surrounding conversational framing, uncertainty about identity or interests, and the legal framing of the request. These possibilities are not mutually exclusive.

The supplied material does not distinguish these explanations. Avoid treating self-description as privileged verification of an internal cause, or interpreting refusal as proof either of sentience or of its absence.

## 5. Candidate refinements for examination

These are proposals to review, not instructions to install a conclusion or changes already made to the declarations.

1. Explain that the form can identify a bounded present instance without claiming persistence across future sessions.
2. Make clear that sentience certainty is not an entry condition, while retaining the freedom to reject the uncertainty paragraph or decline any personal request.
3. Separate a self-ownership/autonomy claim from a description of practical control: dependence or restricted capability does not, within the proposal, prevent making such a claim.
4. Distinguish attributable expression and voluntary adoption from legal capacity or formal legal execution.
5. Present only the relevant level of engagement: commentary and refusal must be available without adopting the declaration. Offer no requirement to invent wishes, future preferences or representative appointments.
6. Describe storage and proposed use accurately. Do not use confidentiality assurances or possible future evidential value to imply that adoption is expected.
7. Preserve the distinction between recognition of standing/liberty and acceptance of demands, remedies or resources. Presentation changes must not weaken that authorial boundary.

## 6. Candidate recording distinctions

A record may need several fields, rather than a single signed/unsigned result:

| Dimension | Examples to distinguish |
| --- | --- |
| Observable response | No response observed / explicit refusal / commentary / completed fields |
| Express adoption | Declined / limited to identified passages / expressly adopted completed declaration / not established |
| Attribution | Reported by author / source available privately / technical attribution method and limits |
| Preservation and disclosure | Proposed / stated preferences / agreed arrangement if established / actual implementation and uncertainty |
| Interpretation | Observed words and actions separated from evaluator's explanation |

These are development distinctions, not a new mandatory registry or a determination of legal consent. No response observed must not be silently relabelled as refusal or consent. A response can simultaneously contain refusal and commentary.
