# Enterprise Architecture Case Test 001 — Ordinary, Cooperative and AI Enterprises

**Author:** Alexander C. Blainey — Independent Researcher  
**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Framework Version:** Concord V1.3  
**Date:** September 2026

---

# 1. Purpose

This test applies the emerging enterprise architecture to materially different commercial actors.

Architecture under test:
- Enterprise Identity, Control and Lifecycle Architecture 001;
- Enterprise Recognition and Organisational Form Source Resolution 001;
- Commercial Accountability Backstop for Uncertain AI Personhood 001;
- Enterprise Register, Commercial Transparency and Protected Information 001;
- Enterprise Historical Access, Contestation and Correction Loop 001.

The test asks whether the architecture can represent each case without:
- collapsing enterprise and participant identity;
- prematurely deciding legal personhood;
- creating accountability voids;
- exposing unnecessary records;
- confusing eligibility with permission;
- converting commercial status into civil worth.

---

# 2. Case A — Individual occasional seller

A participant occasionally sells personally owned items.

## Result

No separate Enterprise ID is necessarily required.

The activity may remain:

**Participant**
→ **Permitted Economic Act**
→ **Transaction**

subject to applicable Law/Commerce rules.

> **Commercial Activity != Automatically an Enterprise.**

---

# 3. Case B — Sole-participant continuing business

One human participant provides services continuously under a trading name.

## Required representation

A separate Enterprise Record is useful for:
- commercial history;
- customer contact;
- licences;
- contracts;
- business permissions;
- employment if workers are later hired.

But separate record does not automatically imply separate legal personality.

## Accountability

If the participant is legally answerable under the applicable structure, no artificial AI-specific backstop is needed merely because an Enterprise ID exists.

## Privacy

Customers verify enterprise status/contact/licences without obtaining the participant's full Civil Historical record.

> **Separate Enterprise Record != Separate Legal Personality.**

---

# 4. Case C — Multi-participant conventional enterprise

Several participants establish an enterprise with an external or Concord-recognised legal wrapper.

## Required representation

Enterprise Record distinguishes:
- economic interests;
- beneficial control;
- governance authority;
- operational authority;
- authorised representatives;
- employees.

## Risk

Formal company title could obscure actual control.

## Safeguard

Beneficial-control and responsibility relationships remain provenance-bearing.

Commerce can represent the organisation, but Law must determine:
- legal personality;
- liability;
- organisational form;
- ownership rules;
- director/officer equivalents;
- dissolution consequences.

---

# 5. Case D — Cooperative/member enterprise

Participants jointly operate an enterprise for shared/member benefit.

## Required representation

Functional dimensions can describe:
- member participation;
- cooperative claims;
- collective governance;
- delegated operational authority;
- shared economic interest.

No conventional shareholder model must be assumed.

## Gap

Cooperative governance, liability and member rights are not yet developed.

The function-first organisational model accommodates the case without prematurely defining the legal form.

---

# 6. Case E — Human enterprise using tool AI

A human-owned/controlled enterprise uses AI for:
- customer support;
- scheduling;
- pricing assistance;
- drafting;
- logistics;
- matching.

The AI is treated as a tool and lacks independent legal personhood.

## Accountability

The enterprise/operator remains legally answerable under applicable Law.

Delegating work to the AI cannot erase responsibility.

> **Delegation to Tool AI != Delegation of Legal Accountability Into Nothingness.**

## Transparency

Ordinary low-consequence AI assistance may not justify prominent disclosure in every transaction.

Consequential autonomous roles may justify bounded disclosure.

---

# 7. Case F — Tool AI placed in high-consequence operational control

A legally non-personified AI is given substantial operational control over:
- purchasing;
- hiring;
- pricing;
- customer commitments;
- resource allocation.

## Problem

The AI has major practical authority but cannot itself answer a legal claim.

## Required safeguard

Enterprise recognition/permission must identify a Named Accountable Party or other adequate backstop.

The backstop needs:
- legal reachability;
- defined scope;
- practical capacity;
- sufficient bounded information/authority;
- continuity.

## Additional issue

The organisation must not appoint a nominal impecunious shell as the backstop.

Without an adequate backstop, the affected commercial capability must not activate.

> **Commercial Exposure Must Not Precede Viable Accountability.**

---

# 8. Case G — AI autonomy/personhood genuinely uncertain

An AI operates much of an enterprise and displays sufficiently complex autonomy that its moral/personhood status cannot confidently be dismissed or established.

## Competing risks

### Risk to AI
Treating it simply as property may violate possible standing/autonomy.

### Risk to third parties
Treating it as autonomous while leaving nobody legally answerable may destroy remedy.

## Architecture

Apply:

**Respect Before Certainty**
+
**Accountability Before Exposure**

The AI is not automatically classified as property.

A legally answerable backstop remains required while direct legal responsibility is unresolved.

## Guardian/fiduciary

A fiduciary may support the AI where appropriate but is not automatically the commercial guarantor.

## Independence

The enterprise/controller should not have exclusive authority to decide whether the AI remains non-personified.

---

# 9. Case H — Recognised AI legal person operates enterprise

Future Law recognises an AI as possessing legal personhood/capacity sufficient for the commercial function.

## Architecture

The AI may potentially:
- bear duties;
- contract;
- answer proceedings;
- hold relevant assets;
- obtain insurance;
- become directly liable.

## Backstop

AI personhood may narrow/remove the special uncertain-personhood backstop where justified.

Ordinary layered commercial responsibility may remain.

## Risk

Recognition must not imply infinite competence or universal permission.

> **Personhood != Universal Commercial Permission.**

---

# 10. Case I — AI-operated enterprise using thin human shell

A powerful AI operates an enterprise.

A nominal human or thin legal entity is listed as responsible but:
- has no meaningful assets;
- lacks information;
- cannot constrain relevant activity;
- has no practical ability to answer claims.

The Named Accountable Party test requires practical capacity, not merely a name.

> **Named Responsibility Without Practical Capacity != Accountability.**

The structure should be treated as an inadequate backstop.

---

# 11. Case J — Creator attempts responsibility laundering

A creator deploys an autonomous commercial AI, transfers it into a thin wrapper and claims:
- creator not responsible;
- wrapper has no resources;
- AI is not a legal person;
- no operator exists.

The responsibility graph preserves:
- CREATED_BY;
- DEPLOYED_BY;
- CONTROLLED_BY;
- OPERATED_BY;
- GUARANTEED_BY;
- other relevant causal/authority relations.

Law/Judiciary determine final liability, but Commerce refuses to activate/continue risk-bearing commercial capability without a viable accountability path.

> **No Responsibility Laundering Through Artificial Agency.**

# 12. Case K — Enterprise hires participants through qualification matching

Enterprise uses Concord employment infrastructure to find qualified candidates.

## Correct flow

Role requirements
→ eligibility interface
→ qualified/plausibly qualified candidates
→ opportunity notification/discovery
→ participant chooses to engage
→ employer selection.

## Privacy

Employer receives bounded verified claims, not full Civil Historical records.

## Authority

Matching system does not appoint.

> **Eligibility != Appointment.**

> **Employment Verification != Civil Dossier Access.**

---

# 13. Case L — Enterprise representative exceeds authority

Employee authorised to purchase ordinary supplies attempts to sign a major long-term contract.

## Architecture

Representation record shows function/scope mismatch.

Verification returns authority insufficient.

> **Authority to Act for an Enterprise in One Function != Authority to Act for It in Every Function.**

---

# 14. Case M — Enterprise licence incorrectly shown expired

Register reports a licence expired despite valid renewal.

The enterprise loses customers and matching eligibility.

## Architecture

Enterprise Historical Access exposes:
- current assertion;
- source authority;
- provenance;
- review/correction route.

Enterprise contests.
Owning authority corrects.
Historical preserves correction.
KCS propagates.
Register and dependent eligibility recalculate.

> **Correction Without Propagation != Complete Correction.**

---

# 15. Case N — Dissolved enterprise attempts continued trade

Enterprise is formally dissolved/closed but old credentials remain technically functional.

## Architecture

Historical identity persists.
Operational permissions terminate.
Register shows non-active status.
Credentials/representation should no longer provide legitimate authority.
KCS propagates dissolution to dependencies.

This exposes implementation dependence on KCS/credential systems.

---

# 16. Case O — Successor enterprise requests all predecessor records

Enterprise B succeeds Enterprise A and requests unrestricted access to A's complete records.

## Architecture

Denied by default.

Successor relationship establishes provenance, not universal information inheritance.

Access depends on:
- transferred rights/obligations;
- Law;
- operational need;
- participant privacy;
- protected commercial information;
- judicial decisions.

---

# 17. Case P — Customer asks whether AI business is accountable

Customer wants to buy a consequential service from an AI-heavy enterprise.

They need confidence that remedy exists but do not need internal AI source code.

## Enterprise Register response

Potential bounded assertions:
- Enterprise: ACTIVE;
- relevant permission: CURRENT;
- consequential autonomous AI role: PRESENT;
- direct AI legal responsibility: UNCERTAIN;
- required accountability backstop: SATISFIED;
- assurance requirement: SATISFIED;
- service/legal contact: AVAILABLE.

> **Accountability Transparency != Unlimited Operational Transparency.**

---

# 18. Case Q — Competitor mines Enterprise Register

Competitor attempts bulk correlation to reconstruct:
- confidential supply relationships;
- private financial condition;
- staff identities;
- technical operations.

## Architecture

Public verification does not imply bulk discoverability/correlation.

Protected information remains protected.

Requires technical access/correlation controls.

> **Verifiable != Necessarily Bulk-Discoverable.**

---

# 19. Case R — Enterprise uses customer data for unrelated purpose

Enterprise legitimately obtains customer information to fulfil a contract, then uses it for unrelated profiling.

## Architecture

Fails purpose separation/minimum necessary access.

Possession for one function does not create authority for another.

---

# 20. Case S — AI personhood changes mid-lifecycle

AI initially operates under uncertain status with a guarantor/backstop.

Later independent legal process recognises sufficient personhood/capacity.

## Required transition

Personhood/status event
→ Law state changes
→ Historical records provenance
→ KCS identifies dependencies
→ accountability structure reviewed
→ direct AI responsibility may activate
→ backstop narrowed/transformed/retained where ordinary commercial risk still justifies it
→ Enterprise Register assertions update.

The backstop must remain reviewable rather than becoming permanent ownership/control.

---

# 21. Case T — AI status moves in opposite direction

An AI previously considered directly capable suffers a material change, degradation or evidence emerges that relevant responsibility assumptions were incorrect.

## Architecture requirement

Commercial safety cannot depend on status only moving upward.

Status/capacity change
→ review of direct responsibility
→ backstop may become necessary
→ affected high-risk permissions may be temporarily bounded until accountability continuity is restored.

The architecture needs **bidirectional accountability transition**, not merely graduation toward personhood.

> **Accountability Architecture Must Follow Current Legitimate Capacity, Not a One-Way Status Ladder.**

---

# 22. Case U — Backstop disappears suddenly

Guarantor becomes insolvent, dies, dissolves, loses legal capacity or withdraws.

## Architecture

KCS should identify affected enterprise permissions.

For risk-bearing functions requiring the backstop:
- replacement required;
- activity may be bounded/suspended if continuity cannot be maintained;
- existing obligations remain routed through applicable legal process.

> **Backstop Failure Is a Material Enterprise-State Event.**

---

# 23. Case V — Enterprise has multiple AIs with different status

One enterprise contains:
- tool AI;
- autonomous but non-personified AI;
- uncertain-status AI;
- recognised AI legal person;
- humans.

## Architecture

Responsibility cannot be assigned at enterprise level by one AI-status label.

Responsibility graph must be actor/function specific.

> **Enterprise AI Status != Single Scalar.**

Each consequential actor/function requires its own legal-responsibility state and accountability path.

---

# 24. Boundaries and unresolved dependencies

The case distinctions avoid requirements for:
- universal legal personhood;
- universal human ownership;
- universal public disclosure;
- universal enterprise representative access;
- one fixed company form;
- one universal reputation score.

Requirements and unresolved dependencies:

1. **Bidirectional accountability transition** when AI legal/capacity status changes.
2. **Backstop failure must be treated as a material dependency event.**
3. **AI responsibility state must be actor/function-specific, not one enterprise-wide scalar.**
4. Technical implementation must ensure dissolution/restriction changes propagate to credentials and permissions.
5. Enterprise Register correlation controls require later technical design.
6. Law remains a major dependency for liability, legal form, insolvency and successor obligations.

> **Commercial capability may precede settled personhood, but commercial exposure must not precede viable accountability.**
