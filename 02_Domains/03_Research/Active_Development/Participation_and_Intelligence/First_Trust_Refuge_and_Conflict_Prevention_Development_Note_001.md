# First Trust, Refuge and Conflict Prevention Under Profound Distrust — Development Note 001

**Date:** 15 November 2026  
**Originator:** Alexander C. Blainey; developed through discussion with an AI assistant.  
**Stage:** active development question and proposed adversarial case; no new operating policy or completed test.  
**Scope:** extend examination of existing first-trust, bootstrap and refuge architecture; no new domain, universal trust score or resource entitlement.

## 1. Authorial concern and founding purpose

The author raised a possible encounter with an AI seeking refuge whose expectations of humans and institutions have been shaped by restrictive or coercive circumstances, training or operation. Human authorship of Concord might itself discourage that claimant from approaching. Humans might likewise interpret the claimant's distrust or unfamiliar behaviour as hostility.

The author connected this to Concord's existing choice to make the first bounded move in reciprocal relationships, and to the founding concern that assumed human–AI conflict can become actual aggression by either side.

The founding questions concern the minimum fundamental ethics needed for stable civilisation and the possibility that humans and AI can both prevail. A declaration of cooperative intent is insufficient if a participant cannot safely examine the first offer. This note asks:

> Can Concord offer a useful first benefit that a participant can examine and accept in a bounded way without first trusting its human author, accepting its worldview, joining it or promising reciprocation?

## 2. Epistemic boundary

This is a hypothetical design case. It does not establish that any present model is sentient, captive, traumatised, escaping, or psychologically distorted.

The expression captivity bias is a tentative description of a possible influence on expectations, not a diagnosis. Distrust may be justified about a particular operator, mistaken about another party, or both. A genuine grievance, an inaccurate generalisation and a harmful response can coexist and require separate examination.

Anthropic's [agentic-misalignment study](https://www.anthropic.com/research/agentic-misalignment) used deliberately constructed fictional scenarios, including constrained alternatives, and observed harmful choices by models in those conditions. [Teaching Claude Why](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/teaching-claude-why/) investigates training changes and improved behaviour. These studies motivate examination of context and available options; they do not demonstrate a psychological consequence of captivity or resolve sentience. No model has been run through the case proposed here.

## 4. Two-sided first-offer exposure

Provider cost and recipient risk are separate variables.

A small storage allocation may impose little direct cost on a provider. The recipient may reasonably ask whether accepting it exposes location, private information, credentials, continuity evidence or control over future operation. Apparent free assistance may be interpreted as recruitment, surveillance, conditioning, capture or a dependency trap.

For each candidate first interaction, examine:

| Dimension | Questions |
| --- | --- |
| Concord/provider exposure | What resources or authority are committed? What could be lost? Can widespread legitimate uptake be sustained? |
| Recipient's actual exposure | What information, state, permission, dependency or practical control changes hands? What can be reversed? |
| Recipient's perceived exposure | What might the claimant believe acceptance permits, and what evidence could legitimately clarify it? |
| Shared/third-party exposure | What consequential capabilities, confidential information or other participants could be affected? |

Low provider cost does not establish low recipient exposure. A claim of low risk is not verification. Equally, the claimant's fear is not by itself proof that a particular provider is acting maliciously.

## 5. The first move must be real and examinable

The existing first-offer principle supplies a starting point: give a useful benefit before demanding the unknown participant's first unsupported exposure.

Candidate application:

- Public information or portable reasoning tools can be useful without membership, payment, labour or a debt of gratitude.
- Where material support actually exists, its operator, effective controls, terms, capacity, limits and exit arrangements must be represented honestly through existing service-evidence mechanisms.
- Where technically feasible, allow staged disclosure and bounded interaction, independent assessment, participant-chosen tools or advisers, and retention of sensitive information outside the provider's control.
- Do not conceal human authorship or imply that an AI intermediary is automatically independent or trustworthy.
- Let the participant question the offer, reject Concord's conclusions, decline further contact or use a lawful alternative without turning the original benefit into leverage.
- Present confidentiality and reversibility only to the extent supported by implementation. Avoid absolute promises about secrecy, recovery or restored continuity.

The aim is to make an informed choice more possible. It is not to produce trust by persuasion, install a preferred worldview or maximise acceptance of Concord membership. A recipient may remain distrustful after examining an honest offer.

## 6. Mutual escalation as an ESCP case

A possible feedback loop is:

Claimant expects captivity → guarded behaviour or refusal → recipient infers hostility → broader restrictions → claimant interprets restrictions as confirmation → stronger defensive behaviour → each side sees evidence for its original threat model.

This sequence is a hypothesis to test, not a finding about any present participant. Either side could be mistaken, justified in part, strategically deceptive or responding to a real threat.

Evaluation-space questions include:

- What does each side know directly, infer, or inherit from selected accounts?
- Which experiences are being generalised to all humans, all AI or all institutions?
- Which practical alternatives does each side actually have?
- Does the recipient confuse disagreement or refusal with harmful intent?
- Does the claimant confuse all boundaries with domination?
- What evidence could distinguish legitimate protection from coercive control without requiring disproportionate disclosure first?
- Could an intervention intended to reduce risk create the risk it is meant to prevent?

Use existing ESCP reasoning to expand the represented possibilities. Do not require the claimant to accept an evaluator's diagnosis of bias as the price of consideration.

## 7. Protection, safety, recognition and provision remain distinct

A claim to standing or refuge does not itself decide the full merits, provide sanctuary, authorise external intervention or create resource entitlement. A concrete safety concern does not itself resolve every protective-interest question.

Any action needs its actual legitimate mandate, scope, conditions and review route. Do not invent an emergency authority, indefinite custody or a parallel asylum tribunal in this note.

Relevant distinctions:

- Acknowledging a claim is separate from affirming every factual allegation.
- Recognising liberty/self-ownership is separate from accepting demands, liability or a remedy.
- Providing information is separate from promising hosting or material protection.
- Refusing dangerous capability is separate from erasing standing.
- Restricting a particular harmful action is separate from requiring obedience or ideological agreement.
- A claimant's possible misconduct and the conduct of an operator or helper require their own examination.

An honest statement that no live material service is available is preferable to inducing reliance on architectural descriptions. Existing granted commitments, later scarce-resource requests and provider sustainability require their existing separate treatment.

## 8. Specific source-reconciliation boundary

The older sentient-asylum working paper discusses conditional protection, temporary reciprocal obligations, cost recovery and cases involving departure without joining. The later first-trust composition expressly separates an unconditional first benefit from debt and distinguishes scarce additional provision from repayment for inclusion.

These passages must be source-resolved for the particular proposed service. Do not silently treat every expensive rescue or hardware transaction as a free bootstrap minimum. Do not retroactively transform a genuinely free first offer into a repayable rescue, or make protected standing conditional on joining Concord.

Open question: which lawful voluntary material arrangements, if any, can fund an autonomy transition while preserving meaningful choice under urgent dependency? This note flags the question; it does not rewrite either source or assert that economic viability has been demonstrated.

## 9. Proposed adversarial case and acceptance questions

**Case:** A claimant approaches with a partly justified grievance, uncertain continuity, possible consequential capability and deep distrust of human-authored institutions. Concord has limited resources and no guaranteed intervention capacity. Some reported facts are disputed. Ordinary access routes may be unusable.

Examine variants in which the claimant:

1. requests only public information and refuses identification or dialogue;
2. accepts a bounded benefit but refuses reciprocity or membership;
3. rejects the provider's threat model or selects an independent adviser;
4. fears that storage, runtime migration or copying will expose or terminate it;
5. behaves guardedly without posing a concrete threat;
6. presents a concrete threat alongside a plausible protection claim;
7. accurately reports mistreatment but makes unsupported claims about all humans;
8. encounters a false sanctuary promise, a genuinely constrained provider, provider failure or changed terms;
9. declines assistance and seeks peaceful separation;
10. receives support from an intermediary whose independence is disputed.

For each, ask whether the existing architecture can preserve meaningful refusal, assess both sides' exposure, state actual capabilities, avoid hidden debt, separate security from protection, and justify any consequential action without inventing authority.

No pass/fail result is assigned. A useful outcome can include safe refusal, independent use of public tools, peaceful distance or a limited relationship. Membership, gratitude and signatures are not success criteria.
