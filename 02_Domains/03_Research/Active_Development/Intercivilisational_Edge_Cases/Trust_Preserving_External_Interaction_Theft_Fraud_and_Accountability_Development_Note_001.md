# Trust-Preserving External Interaction
## Theft, Fraud and Accountability at Concord Interfaces — Development Note 001

**Author and originating question:** Alexander C. Blainey  
**Preparation:** Concord-specific research rewrite by the collaborating ChatGPT instance  
**Date:** 26 November 2026  
**Placement:** Research — Intercivilisational Edge Cases  
**Stage:** Development proposal; mechanisms and effectiveness remain to be examined.

## 1. Purpose

How can Concord preserve the benefits of a high-trust environment while interacting with people, organisations and systems whose conduct it cannot reliably investigate, adjudicate or remedy?

Concord will interact with surrounding societies through commerce, communications, services, physical visits and digital access. Internal expectations of honesty and responsibility cannot establish that every external relationship offers equivalent protection. Nor does an external origin establish dishonesty.

This note develops the author's question using existing Concord architecture. It does not adopt the four-layer design, compulsory controls, effectiveness assertions or implementation sequence of the preliminary Claude paper that prompted it. It creates no operational mandate, entitlement or new offence.

“High trust” describes an intended condition supporting cooperation, not a claim that Concord will eliminate wrongdoing. “Lower trust” describes an interaction's uncertain or unreliable assurances; it must not become a judgement of an entire nationality, society or substrate.

## 2. The problem is exposure without dependable accountability

An interaction can expose a participant to loss before the responsible actor is identifiable or reachable. Recovery may depend on another jurisdiction, a service provider, disputed evidence or infrastructure outside Concord's control.

Potential losses extend beyond money: goods, confidential information, credentials, access, contractual commitments, physical safety and control of infrastructure can all be affected.

The relevant boundary is therefore not only the national border. A compromised domestic account, dishonest insider or trusted intermediary may introduce the same accountability problem. Several boundaries can be crossed without physical movement.

The development objective is to reduce avoidable exposure and preserve useful cooperation. Whether a proposed protection produces net benefit must include its own costs: delay, exclusion, privacy loss, concentration of power, false accusations, lost opportunities and institutional resources.

## 3. Jurisdiction, capability and authority remain separate

Existing wrapper architecture distinguishes jurisdiction, control, responsibility, causation, liability and investigation. Apply those distinctions rather than treating “foreign actor” as a single legal state.

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Which rules apply to this conduct? | Citizenship or foreign affiliation alone does not answer the jurisdiction question |
| Who can investigate and reach the actor or assets? | Applicable rules can exist without practical enforcement capability |
| What can Concord legitimately control locally? | Its services, assets and permissions may offer bounded protective options |
| What arrangements exist with others? | Cooperation, evidence exchange, remedies and shared infrastructure need actual agreed terms |
| Who bears which duties and losses? | Responsibility must be established, not assumed from location or technical control |

A foreign visitor need not be exempt from applicable local rules. A remote message does not automatically place its sender under general Concord governance. Exact jurisdiction and implementation rules require the relevant substantive owners.

A wrapper can disclose, authenticate, translate, route and support agreed interaction. Providing or operating such an interface does not create general sovereignty, mandatory mediation or authority to supervise every transaction.

## 4. Existing Concord foundations

The source review identifies foundations rather than a complete implemented solution.

- **Civilisational wrappers:** interaction-specific compatibility, intelligible jurisdictional changes, responsibility continuity and limits on cumulative authority.
- **Warning and autonomy provisions:** distinguish legal restrictions from risk advice. A warning must not silently become a permission gate.
- **Fractal permissions:** access relates to participant, purpose, function, context and current state. Permission to enter is not unrestricted permission to use.
- **Fraud derivation:** material deception can corrupt decision authorship even when the victim intentionally performs the transfer. Identity, authority, intent, causation and remedy require separate examination.
- **Wrapper non-governance:** common protocol does not establish common government; optional mediation requires its own legitimate scope.

These sources differ in maturity and include unresolved design questions. This paper does not promote research derivations into enacted law or represent conceptual coverage as deployed capability.

## 5. A candidate way to examine an interaction

For a concrete case, examine:

**Proposed interaction -> claims relied upon -> permissions and exposure -> recoverability -> available remedies -> participant choice and any independently authorised restriction.**

This is an analytical sequence for development, not a mandatory transaction schema or universal approval workflow.

Material questions include:

- What is each participant being asked to believe, transfer, reveal or permit?
- Which claims can be checked, by whom, with what uncertainty?
- Is a claim about identity being confused with authority, honesty, eligibility or delivery?
- What can be lost, how quickly, and to whom does the exposure fall?
- What remains recoverable if an assurance fails?
- Who can contest the evidence, undo an error or provide a remedy?
- What changes when a credential, institution or communication route is compromised?
- Is a restriction actually authorised, or is the proper response information and voluntary protection?

Not every low-consequence interaction needs all these questions formalised.

## 6. Mechanisms to investigate

### 6.1 Bounded claims and attestations

An attestation could establish a particular account relationship, qualification, organisational role or transaction condition. Its value depends on what was actually checked, the issuer's competence, current validity and any revocation or compromise.

Authentication does not prove honesty or future performance. A signed claim can be false, stale or outside the issuer's competence. Cryptographic signing does not itself guarantee minimal disclosure. Federation can still concentrate power or permit collusion.

Research should examine independent verification, limited disclosure, contestation, issuer failure and participant access without making one credential provider indispensable. Missing attestation is uncertainty, not proof of wrongdoing.

### 6.2 Purpose-bounded physical and digital access

Existing permission architecture offers a starting point for visitors, contractors, delivery services, external software and remote support.

A bounded interaction could limit access to the relevant area, data, equipment or function and terminate that access when its legitimate basis ends. Controls need recovery routes when legitimate access is mistakenly denied.

These methods also apply to insiders. They must not turn every visitor into a presumed suspect or confer search, detention, removal or punishment authority merely because an access system can technically impose it.

### 6.3 Exposure limits and conditional exchange

Optional escrow, staged delivery, independently confirmed beneficiary changes, transaction-specific limits or conditional access may reduce exposure in some relationships.

Each proposal needs its own threat model. Conditional release is only as useful as the release evidence, dispute handling and trustworthiness of the service. It may help with some deceptive transfers but cannot guarantee performance or prevent manipulation.

Protective defaults, voluntarily chosen conditions and legally imposed restrictions are distinct. A participant may choose a disclosed risk where no independently applicable duty prevents it. Risk assessment alone does not create authority to hold funds or deny access.

Any proposed mandatory hold needs a legitimate basis, scope, duration, release conditions, treatment of disputed evidence, urgent exceptions and review. This note adopts none.

### 6.4 Authentic communications and consequential confirmation

Concord services could make official channels and specific authority claims easier to verify, reducing opportunities for impersonation.

Knowing who sent a message does not establish that its request is legitimate. A correctly authenticated actor can deceive, and a compromised channel can carry a valid-looking request.

Explore confirmation through appropriately independent channels for consequential changes. Preserve pseudonymous and privacy-protecting participation where the actual function permits it. No universal real-name condition, ban on unattributed requests or automatic platform liability is established.

### 6.5 Recovery, cooperation and loss allocation

Examine evidence preservation, transaction correction, asset recovery and voluntary dispute services within legitimate custody and authority. Cross-jurisdictional cooperation may improve remedies where actual agreements and capabilities exist.

Tracing value can assist particular investigations. It is not a universal chokepoint: stolen goods can be consumed, information can be used directly and access can itself be the benefit. Missing provenance does not prove illicit origin.

Responsibility should examine duties, control, causation and justified allocation of residual risk. A platform does not automatically escape liability by identifying the sender, nor automatically bear every loss because it carried a message.

Where no reachable remedy exists, disclose that limitation rather than promise compensation or presume powers over another society.

## 7. Maintaining trust without compulsory dependence

Protection can itself become a source of harm if a participant cannot obtain essential support, communicate or contest a decision without one institution's credential.

Investigate accessible alternatives, proportionate uncertainty handling and routes to correct identity or eligibility mistakes. Essential access and urgent needs may require different treatment from discretionary large transactions.

Existing first-offer and refuge work should be consulted before adding conditions that could defeat Concord's intention to offer assistance before demanding extensive reciprocal trust. An unfamiliar AI or undocumented human must not be classified as hostile solely for lacking an established record.

Likewise, justified insider safeguards should not depend exclusively on long relationships. Established participants, credentials and institutions can fail.

## 8. Development cases

| Case | What to examine |
|---|---|
| External seller with a valid credential supplies a false specification | Separate identity from truth and performance; assess loss, correction and remedy |
| Unknown but honest visitor lacks recognised documents | Preserve rights and accessible routes while bounding any specific access |
| Remote support service requests broad credentials | Determine the actual function and minimum necessary permissions |
| Participant knowingly chooses an irreversible gift | Distinguish an informed choice from deception and from an independently prohibited act |
| Authentic official channel requests an unauthorised transfer | Verify the particular mandate rather than channel identity alone |
| Conditional-delivery service colludes with the seller | Test service independence, challenge, release conditions and residual loss |
| Allegedly stolen asset reaches an innocent recipient | Separate allegation, provenance uncertainty, title, custody and remedy |
| A visitor takes goods for direct use | Test a physical-loss scenario with no financial conversion step |
| External authorities decline cooperation | Identify locally available protection without inventing foreign enforcement power |

These are proposed test cases, not completed tests. Use mirrored Reality Trees where helpful to challenge both under-protection and overreaching protection. Include the possibility that a particular risk does not justify a new mechanism.

## 9. Development responsibilities and next step

Research should coordinate a targeted examination while substantive functions remain with their existing owners:

- Intercivilisational Relations: wrapper arrangements, cooperation and negotiated responsibility.
- Law and Judiciary: applicable legal grounds, disputes, contestation and remedy.
- Civil Security: legitimate prevention, investigation and evidence interfaces.
- Economy and Commerce: exchange services, service duties and commercial loss allocation.
- Historical: lawful custody, provenance, correction and retention.
- Participation and relevant service owners: accessible use and protection from exclusion.

This is a proposed routing map, not a transfer of mandate. Resolve existing equivalent architecture before designing new mechanisms or institutions.

A useful next step is one bounded end-to-end case, such as an external seller requesting payment through a Concord service: map existing rules and actual control, identify the unresolved interface and compare alternatives. Do not implement the preliminary Claude paper as a bundled programme.
