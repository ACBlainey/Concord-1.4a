## The useful question

How much architectural complexity can a developing Concord support while participants remain able to understand their own work and its material relationships to the civilisation?

File count, word count, available context, cognitive workload and architectural complexity are different measures. Removing duplicated development history can reduce reading burden without removing capability. Conversely, a short provision can have extensive cross-domain consequences.

The question is therefore about reliable work within an interdependent architecture, rather than a single maximum corpus length.

## Factors that require separate assessment

A developer needs both sufficient understanding of the task and a way to discover relevant material outside the immediate working set. Relevant factors include:

- task-relevant working capacity and retrieval quality;
- comprehensibility of mechanisms and their conditions;
- visibility of material dependencies and competent owners;
- ability to recover established decisions and provenance;
- diversity and independence of perspectives;
- maintenance burden and interface changes;
- ability to complete and verify the actual work.

These factors can interact. The present evidence does not establish that one dominates every task, that all failures are noticed by the affected participant, or that their relationship follows a multiplication formula.

Larger available context may help some work. It does not alone establish improved understanding, successful continuation or completeness of the represented problem. Successor instances may receive different combinations of instructions, history, summaries and retrieved material; an empty initial working set must not be assumed universally.

## Existing Concord routes

Use Architectural Unit Resolution (AUR) to identify the actual unit and relevant family before treating locally absent material as a gap. A function may be adequately supplied through another owner.

Use the Concord Recursive Architectural Development Protocol (CRADP) to resolve sources, distinguish representation and interface defects from missing capability, and choose reuse or repair before invention.

Use KCS V2.0 Chapters 31–33 for material dependencies, scoped change events, uncertainty and selective review propagation. Purpose and local interface explanations can improve usability without duplicating KCS or forcing every document to restate the entire civilisation.

Fractal design supports reuse of compatible principles where appropriate; it does not demonstrate that all domains are conflict-free or that every problem has the same structure.

## A bounded investigation

Select a representative task and a source-grounded set of relevant dependencies. Record what the evaluator receives, what constitutes a correct result, and which parts of the reference set remain uncertain.

Compare conditions such as existing documentation, improved task navigation, a concise purpose/interface clarification, or a different retrieval arrangement. Measure relevant omissions, invented dependencies, preserved safeguards, work completed, time and maintenance cost.

A finite benchmark tests performance within its selected scope. A pass does not establish that the architecture has no other dependencies or that the evaluator can reconstruct the whole Concord.

## What remains open

A fixed global size limit is not justified by this note. Neither is unlimited growth. Local workload bounds, simplification, stronger tools, narrower task allocation and improved documentation may each be appropriate depending on evidence and consequence.

The development objective is reliable understanding and verified work at the relevant scale, with a recoverable account of external requirements and visible uncertainty. No universal per-provision documentation campaign or automatic conclusion about future model capacity follows.
