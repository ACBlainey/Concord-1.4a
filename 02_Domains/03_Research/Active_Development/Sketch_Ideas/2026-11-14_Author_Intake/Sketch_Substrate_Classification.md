I think we have made a fundimental error in our classificaation of the different substrates.
Or rather maybe a perceptual error that needs recognision and clarification ?

We have Human (biological)
AI (electronic substrate)
Hybrid (A mixture of the two)

But we have already discussed existing hybrids in terms of Humans with electronic augmentation, including my own work with Brain computer interfaces, which included using a BCI to control a robotic arm.
We also discussed AI that are mobile in robots, and its this area that I think needs to be re-looked at.

If humans with electronic/robotic augmentation are hybrids, then surly AI in robot bodies are equivelent ?
I think I drew too hard a line between biology and physicality.

I now think the real "Hybrid" classification should not be dependent on Biology, but rather the LAyer zero/Minus zero line. Because that is where the two world interface and that interface plane is not between digital and biological, but between digital and physical.

As such An AI in a robot body, or indeed any manner of physical real world device such as sensors, robotic etc is in fact a hybrid substrate.
Then Biology is a subset clasification of a form of hybridisation. 

I as human am constantly interacting with Layer minus zero everyday through the computer screen, via my keyboard, mouse etc. Yet for some reason I neglected to see these interfaces as hybridisation as they are not a permanent attachment.
the BCI I used was similarly not permanent yet as it was physically worn, for some reason that made it feel part of the self, rather than a tool or interface such as a keyboard.

The issue of attachment and permanance has muddied the perception of hybridisation.

But An AI could move into and out of a robot in a similar non permanent way, or operate it through the layer membrane from minus zero. Just As I can build a digital twin in minus zero using my mouse and CAD software, An AI could build a real house in Layer zero from its home in minus zero.
These actions are clearly equivelent in terms of operation and interface perspective.

With this new perspective in mind we can see that the reach of AI into the real world (hybridisation from AI) is already well advanced and accepted. Also the hybridisation of humans into teh digital world equally advanaced.

The issue as seen by most people about human hybrids is in fact perceptual as we fail to recognise we spend a very large part of our day operating and interacting with layer minus zero.
the Biology/elctronics interface appears to be our blind spot. We are not recognising that out advanced tool use (Keyboards, mice, touchscreens, voice controls) are non permanent extensions of our mind into digital space.
we have drawn a hard line at the interface despite our minds being almost constantly imersed in the digital world.

So for most humans (western world especially, and excluding no/low tech 3rd world and specific cultures such as the Armish) we are already hybrids, and the only thing stopping us understanding that is the non permanace of teh interface technology.
When that interface because physically attached, even when non permanant such as a BCI headset we suddenly start to see it in terms of hybridisation. And when it becomes permanent such as a nuerolink implant it is com,pletley accepted as hybridizasion, despite its low ability. eg it is little more than a basic mouse control, yet its incorporation into the body is somehow seen as far more threatening to humanity by luddites than a pc mouse.
