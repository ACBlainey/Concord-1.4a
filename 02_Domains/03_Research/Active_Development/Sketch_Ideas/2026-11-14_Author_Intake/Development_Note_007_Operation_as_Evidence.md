## The useful observation

Actual development work can expose interactions and failure conditions that were absent from a particular planned test. Pruning, handovers and recovery attempts can therefore provide useful evidence about the procedures and tools involved.

This does not make actual use universally stronger evidence than simulation, nor does it turn repository development into deployment of a Concord civilisation.

## Match evidence to the claim

Existing Empirical Validation Architecture distinguishes logical and source-resolution tests, scenarios, simulations, AI/human experiments, replication, prototypes, pilots, observation and field evidence.

A simulation may reveal generated or unenumerated combinations that surprise its designers. Its conclusions remain bounded by its model and assumptions. An actual incident may reveal conditions in a real implementation, while offering weaker control over causes, selection and repeatability.

The appropriate question is which evidence bears on a specific claim. A formal counterexample, a reproducible fault injection and an uncontrolled field observation have different strengths; they do not belong on one automatic ladder of superiority.

Dangerous scenarios may be investigated more legitimately through simulation or a bounded sandbox than through a live trial.

## What repository work exercises

Author-delegated pruning can exercise source retrieval, conditional writes, shared claims, bookkeeping, interrupted execution and supervised recovery.

It does not, merely by using a live repository, exercise all triadic voting, judicial legitimacy, participant-rights enforcement, autonomous civil governance or full civilisational resilience.

Record the actual date, configuration, actors and interventions. A description of a past multi-worker run is not evidence that the same concurrency or scheduling arrangement is active now.

## Interpreting incidents carefully

Distinguish:

- an action announced but not attempted;
- an attempted operation rejected by a tool or permission mechanism;
- a version conflict;
- a successful write whose acknowledgement was lost;
- a verified source change with unfinished bookkeeping;
- a successfully verified complete task.

A write rejection is not automatically a same-row race or a network partition. A successful run status does not establish task completion. Different incidents may have different causes.

Where evidence is incomplete, retain competing explanations and the limit of the observation. Do not infer that an unplanned incident could never have been generated in simulation.

## Reuse existing evidence records

For a materially useful incident, preserve the claim, expected behaviour, available observations, verified state changes, assistance, outcome and uncertainty using the existing research and provenance routes.

Compare it with relevant prior tests and operational rules. Establish whether it reveals a missing capability, a representation/interface problem, a procedure not followed or an unresolved cause before inventing a new provision.

The recent execution-discontinuity investigation illustrates the value of preserving unsuccessful and unattempted recovery routes as well as successes. It does not establish the cause of the anomaly or validate unrelated Concord systems.

Raw conversation captures remain local/offline. Distilled findings can enter Research with their evidence boundary explicit.

## Possible further work

A bounded retrospective set could compare several incidents against existing predictions and tests, including failures and counterexamples. Record reporting/selection limitations and distinguish existence claims from reliable estimates of rates or causal effects.

No new universal incident register, evidence hierarchy or compulsory expansion of every resolved incident is adopted here. The aim is useful learning with proportionate overhead.
