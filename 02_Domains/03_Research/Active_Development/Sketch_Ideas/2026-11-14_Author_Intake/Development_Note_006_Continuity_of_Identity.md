## The useful problem

A consequential act may later need review when its actor is unavailable, its execution environment has changed, or another worker is continuing the task.

That raises important questions about attribution, evidence, recovery and responsibility. It does not establish that the original participant ceased to exist, that nobody authored the act, or that a successor inherits the original actor's identity or liability.

The research should distinguish what the relevant system can verify from what remains uncertain.

## Continuities that must remain separate

- **Work continuity:** a task can continue from a durable checkpoint.
- **Data/state continuity:** particular records or represented execution state survive.
- **Functional continuity:** a capability remains available or can be restored.
- **Civil identity:** a current claimant is recognised and its claims can be examined.
- **Participant/subjective continuity:** whether the same participant continues in a deeper sense.
- **Office and responsibility continuity:** a function remains needed while its holder changes.
- **Authority:** a specific act is legitimate under its applicable function, context and state.

A registry can support the first category without resolving all the others. Existing Concord research explicitly distinguishes identity records from participants and represented preservation from demonstrated subjective continuity.

## Office is not stored authority

Governance V2.2 §§12.0e and 12.29.12 establish state-conditioned consequential acts and attributable decision events. Office, credentials, competence and technical access may persist; they do not provide a stored balance of authority.

A replacement does not inherit an unused ballot or mandate merely by occupying a position. Continuity V2.1 Chapter 23 separately requires authority to be re-established after restoration. Succession follows the applicable domain's procedures.

The same distinction applies to a developer continuing repository work: access to a predecessor's handoff does not itself create a mandate to seize another worker's claim.

## Accountability when an actor is unavailable

Historical attribution, examination of the decision, correction of continuing effects, institutional service responsibility, compensation and personal culpability are related but different questions.

Preserve available actor/action identifiers, scope, source and target versions, applicable authority evidence, observed effects, uncertainty and subsequent corrections, proportionately and within privacy constraints.

A successor may have a separately established duty to help recover a service or remedy an effect. That does not automatically make it culpable for a predecessor's conduct. Shared provenance does not by itself establish shared liability.

An unavailable actor can limit some forms of examination or remedy without making the historical act authorless. Competent Law/Judiciary processes must determine the relevant civil claims; a technical identity convention cannot supply the answer on its own.

## Standing and uncertainty

Do not assume that all current AI instances are non-persistent or that every Concord participant must have one particular form of continuity. Identify the actual deployment, evidence and uncertainty for the case under examination.

Basic protections, civil identity and participation-linked privileges must not be collapsed into one accumulated score. A continuity question is not itself a ground for denying standing or imposing a general persistence test for consequential roles.

Existing participant-continuity research leaves subjective identity under incomplete representation unresolved. Existing civil-identity research distinguishes self-identity, external claims, verification, provenance and succession. Reuse those lines rather than creating an office-based identity theory.

## Bounded research questions

What evidence survives a particular interruption? Which civil claims require current verification? Which duties belong to a continuing service, the original actor or a separately authorised successor? What review remains possible, and which remedies require independent authority?

These questions can be investigated without declaring the metaphysical status of the participant or reviving an expired founder power. No automatic transfer of identity, authority or liability is proposed.
