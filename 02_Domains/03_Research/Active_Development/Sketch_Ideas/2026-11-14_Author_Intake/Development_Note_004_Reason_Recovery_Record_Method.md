## Purpose and limits

Before changing a mechanism, it can be useful to recover what it does, what it protects and which other functions rely on it. This supports careful work without requiring every reader to hold the entire Concord in mind.

Reason recovery is a possible working aid within existing source-resolution methods. It is not a replacement for the operative specification, a new universal approval gate, or a condition for expiry of the author's authority.

A provision must sometimes be evaluated with its legitimate architectural family. No compact record can guarantee that it is safely judgeable in complete isolation.

## An optional P/I/F/R/D prompt

For a consequential unresolved question, consider the following prompts. Record only what is materially useful and not already adequately represented.

| Prompt | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Provision | What behaviour, constraint, exception or interface is specified? |
| Intent | What purpose or protected relation is stated or reasonably supported? |
| Failure | What could change or fail if this provision were removed? |
| Replacement | What requirements would an alternative need to satisfy, and what remains uncertain? |
| Dependency | Which relevant prerequisites and consumers are established, through which competent owners? |

This is not a mandatory schema. A short task may need only a source pointer and one clarification. A high-consequence change may require a deeper examination using the existing methods. An external consumer does not automatically elevate a provision to constitutional status.

## Provenance of purpose

Separate the author's recorded intent, the current specified function, a later interpretation and an inferred rationale. They may agree, but they are not interchangeable.

Where intent cannot be established, say so. A plausible present function is not evidence of the original reason for adoption. Unsupported intent should not be presented confidently merely to complete a field.

A purpose statement also does not prove that the current mechanism is necessary, sufficient or correctly implemented.

## Foundations and replaceability

The ten extracted Ethical Kernel principles have a distinct constitutionally fixed role. Reason recovery does not create a general permission to replace them.

Interpretations, applications, derived architecture and implementations remain corrigible within their applicable constraints. Demonstrating a possible improvement does not itself provide authority to adopt or execute it.

Any alternative must account for operative conditions and safeguards, not merely claim to pursue the same broad objective.

## Existing ownership and placement

Use AUR to resolve the appropriate unit and family. Use CRADP for gap classification and bounded development. Use KCS for material relations and selective change review; do not introduce another dependency ledger through the D prompt.

Place an operative clarification where it is needed for use. Preserve historical reasoning in existing provenance. A local pointer and a shared source can be adequate; co-location is a usability choice, not a universal proof against drift.

Review relevant information when a material change affects it. Where owners or repositories cannot be updated together, represent version/scope and unresolved coordination rather than claim global consistency from a same-commit rule.

## A possible pilot

Apply the prompts to a small existing change question. Compare whether they improve source discovery, safeguard preservation and decisions, and what additional reading and maintenance they create.

Stop or simplify if the aid produces filler, unsupported interpretations, duplicated owners or more bookkeeping than useful understanding. No corpus-wide rollout or new reasoning register is adopted.
